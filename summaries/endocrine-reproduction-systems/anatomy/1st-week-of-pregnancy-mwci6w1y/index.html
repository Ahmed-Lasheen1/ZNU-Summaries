Basic terminology and periods

Source: page 55

Embryology is the branch of science that studies the formation and development of an organism.

  • Growth is an increase in the number and size of cells.
  • Differentiation is the specialization of the tissues of different organs to perform special functions.
  • Development involves both growth and differentiation.

Clinicians divide prenatal development into first, second and third trimesters of 3 months each. Embryologically it is divided into 3 periods:

Germinal period
First two weeks after fertilization
Embryonic period
3rd until the end of the 8th week (2nd month)
Fetal period (organ growth)
3rd month until the end of pregnancy

Fertilization

Source: pages 57 to 59
Definition

Fertilization is the fusion of a mature sperm with an ovum (haploid germ cells) to form a single zygote (diploid cell). It usually occurs in the lateral 1/3 of the uterine tube, the ampulla.

Preparation of sperms

  • Capacitation converts immature spermatozoa to mature ones by removing the glycoprotein coat covering the sperm head. It takes 7 hours and occurs in the female reproductive tract (uterus and uterine tubes).
  • Capacitation increases sperm motility and makes the sperm able to penetrate the outer layers of the egg. Spermatozoa are artificially capacitated for in vitro fertilization (IVF).
  • The acrosomal reaction is the release of the acrosomal cap contents (acrosin, hyaluronidase and a trypsin-like substance), needed to pass through the corona radiata and penetrate the zona pellucida.

Phases of fertilization

  1. Passing through the corona radiata

    Of 300 to 500 sperms reaching the site of fertilization, only one fertilizes the ovum. The rest only help it pass through the corona radiata.

  2. Penetration of the zona pellucida

    Done with the aid of enzymes released from the acrosomal cap.

  3. Fusion of oocyte and sperm cell membranes

    The head of the spermatozoon enters the cytoplasm of the oocyte.

Response of the oocyte

  • Cortical and zona reactions: cortical receptors alter their structure and the zona pellucida changes in structure and composition to become impermeable, which prevents polyspermy.
  • The secondary oocyte undergoes the second meiotic division and gives rise to an ovum.

Results of fertilization

  • The sperm head swells to form the male pronucleus and the ovum nucleus forms the female pronucleus (23 chromosomes each). They unite to form one nucleus of 46 chromosomes, and the fertilized ovum is now a zygote.
  • Sex is determined.
  • Cleavage is initiated: fertilization provides energy for the repeated cell divisions of the zygote. Without fertilization the oocyte degenerates.

Cleavage and blastocyst formation

Source: pages 59 to 60

Cleavage is the repeated mitotic division of the zygote into smaller cells called blastomeres. It lasts up to 6 to 7 days (until implantation), beginning in the ampulla of the uterine tube and ending in the uterine cavity, where the conceptus reaches the site of implantation.

Zygote
2-cell, 4-cell stages
Morula (16 cells, day 3)
Blastocyst (day 4)

The 16-cell morula is surrounded by the zona pellucida. It enters the uterine cavity around day 4. Fluid then enters between the blastomeres and forms a cavity, the blastocele, and this stage is the blastocyst.

Trophoblast

  • The outer cell mass
  • Its cells flatten

Embryoblast

  • The inner cell mass
  • Lies at the embryonic pole
High-yield

The zona pellucida starts to degenerate by the end of the 4th day and disappears by the end of the 5th day, allowing implantation to begin.

Implantation and the decidua

Implantation is the embedding of the blastocyst into the uterine endometrium. It begins on the 6th or 7th day after fertilization and is complete by the 12th day. The normal site is the midline of the upper part of the body of the uterus, in the anterior or posterior wall, near the fundus.

The blastocyst fixes its embryonic pole to the implantation site. The trophoblast proliferates rapidly into two layers:

Syncytiotrophoblast

  • Outer layer
  • Cytoplasm with randomly dispersed nuclei, no cell membrane
  • Phagocytic: makes a pit in the endometrium

Cytotrophoblast

  • Inner layer
  • Cells with well-defined walls

The blastocyst gradually sinks into the pit until it is completely embedded in the endometrium. Key days:

Day 6 or 7
Implantation begins
Day 9
Site of penetration closed by a fibrin plug
Day 11
Surface epithelium overgrows the fibrin plug
Day 12
Blastocyst completely embedded

The decidua

The endometrium of pregnancy after implantation is called the decidua. It has three parts in relation to the blastocyst:

Decidua capsularis
Between the blastocyst and the uterine cavity
Decidua basalis
Between the blastocyst and the wall of the uterus
Decidua parietalis
Lines the remaining wall of the uterus

Abnormal sites of implantation

Source: pages 63 to 64

Implantation away from the usual site is abnormal (ectopic) implantation and results in ectopic pregnancy. It is classified by site:

Abnormal implantation
  • Uterine: placenta previa lower uterine segment
    • Lateralis lateral wall of the body of the uterus
    • Marginalis covers the internal os partially
    • Centralis covers the internal os completely
  • Tubal in the uterine tube; the most common extrauterine site
  • Abdominal rare; usually the ovary or mesentery
Clinical relevance

Implantation outside the uterine cavity causes an ectopic pregnancy, which cannot reach full term because of inadequate nutrition.

Key Points / Quick Review

Periods

  • Germinal: first 2 weeks after fertilization.
  • Embryonic: 3rd to end of 8th week.
  • Fetal: 3rd month to end of pregnancy.

Fertilization

  • Site: ampulla, lateral 1/3 of the uterine tube.
  • Capacitation (7 hours) then acrosomal reaction.
  • Three phases: corona radiata, zona pellucida, membrane fusion.
  • Cortical and zona reactions prevent polyspermy.
  • Pronuclei (23 each) unite to give a 46-chromosome zygote.

Cleavage and blastocyst

  • Cleavage lasts up to 6 to 7 days; the 16-cell morula forms about day 3.
  • Blastocyst: trophoblast (outer) and embryoblast (inner, embryonic pole).
  • The zona pellucida disappears by the end of day 5.

Implantation and decidua

  • Starts day 6 or 7; fibrin plug day 9; epithelium overgrows day 11; complete day 12.
  • Syncytiotrophoblast (outer, no cell membrane) and cytotrophoblast (inner).
  • Decidua: capsularis, basalis, parietalis.

Abnormal implantation

  • Placenta previa: lateralis, marginalis, centralis.
  • Tubal is the most common extrauterine site.
  • Ectopic pregnancy cannot reach full term (inadequate nutrition).
