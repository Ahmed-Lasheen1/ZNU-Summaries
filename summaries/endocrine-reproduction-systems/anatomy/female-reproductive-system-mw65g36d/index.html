The uterus: description and parts

The uterus is a hollow, thick-walled, muscular organ lying in the pelvic cavity between the urinary bladder in front and the rectum behind.

  • Dimensions: 3 x 2 x 1 inches, which is 7.5 cm long, 5 cm broad and 2.5 cm thick.
  • Weight: 30 to 40 gm.
  • Shape: piriform (pear-shaped), with its apex directed downward and backward.

Parts

Fundus
Part above the level of the opening of the uterine tubes
Body
Part from the fundus to the isthmus
Cervix
Part from the isthmus to the lower end, projecting into the vagina

The isthmus is a slight constriction on the outer surface midway between the fundus and the cervix.

The vagina divides the cervix into a supravaginal and a vaginal portion. The vaginal portion opens into the vagina by the external os.

Cervical canal

  • Internal os: communicates with the cavity of the body.
  • External os: opens into the vagina.

Peritoneal covering and position

Peritoneal covering

The peritoneum covers the fundus from its posterior, superior and anterior surfaces. In front it continues onto the anterior surface of the body. Behind it continues over the posterior surface of the body, the cervix and the upper third of the vagina.

Utero-vesical pouch

  • Formed at the isthmus, where peritoneum is reflected onto the superior surface of the bladder
  • Contains loops of small intestine

Recto-vaginal (Douglas) pouch

  • Formed where peritoneum is reflected from the vagina to cover the middle and upper thirds of the rectum
  • Contains loops of small intestine

The anterior and posterior peritoneal layers from the side wall of the uterus come together to form the broad ligament, which connects the uterus with the lateral pelvic wall.

Position of the uterus

The normal position is anteverted and anteflexed.

  • Anteversion: the long axis of the uterus is bent forwards on the long axis of the vagina, forming an angle of about 90 degrees.
  • Anteflexion: the long axis of the body is bent forwards at the isthmus on the long axis of the cervix, forming an obtuse angle.

High-yield

The ligamentum transversale colli (also called Mackenrodt, cardinal or lateral ligament) is the main ligament supporting the uterus.

Course of the round ligament

  1. Attachment

    It is attached to the fundus below and in front of the uterine tubes.

  2. Course in the pelvis

    It passes forward, upward and laterally between the two layers of the broad ligament and crosses over the external iliac vessels.

  3. Termination

    It passes through the inguinal canal to be attached to the labium majus.

The broad ligament

The free upper border encloses the uterine tube. Because the uterus is bent forwards over the bladder, this border is really antero-superior.

Borders

  • Antero-superior: encloses the uterine tube.
  • Postero-inferior: the two layers diverge at the pelvic floor; the anterior continues forwards, the posterior continues backwards on the pelvic floor then upwards on the middle third of the rectum.
  • Lateral: the layers diverge on the lateral pelvic wall.
  • Medial: continues with the peritoneum on the anterior and posterior surfaces of the uterus.

Blood supply of the uterus

Arterial

  • Uterine artery: a branch of the internal iliac artery. It runs a tortuous course on the lateral wall of the uterus, then passes laterally along the uterine tube within the broad ligament to anastomose with the ovarian artery.
  • Ovarian artery: a branch of the abdominal aorta.

Venous

The veins form two plexuses, one on each side of the lateral wall. Two veins arise from each plexus and end in the internal iliac vein.

The vagina

The vagina is a muscular tube extending upward and backward from the vulva to the uterus, with its anterior and posterior walls normally in contact.

  • Length: 7.5 cm along the anterior wall and 9 cm along the posterior wall.
  • The cervix pierces the upper part of the anterior wall and projects downwards and backwards into the vagina.
  • Its long axis forms a right angle with the long axis of the uterus (anteversion).
  • The upper half lies above the pelvic floor and the lower half lies in the perineum.
  • In a virgin the orifice is partly closed by the hymen, which is perforated in its center to allow passage of menstrual blood.

Fornices

The vaginal lumen around the cervix is divided into four fornices: anterior (in front of the cervix), posterior (behind) and two lateral (one on each side).

High-yield

The posterior fornix is the deepest. It is covered by peritoneum of the recto-uterine pouch (of Douglas), the only part of the vagina with a peritoneal covering.

The ureter is first adjacent to the lateral fornix, then passes across the front of the anterior fornix to enter the bladder.

Blood supply

  • Arterial: the vaginal artery (branch of the internal iliac artery) and the vaginal branch of the uterine artery.
  • Venous: a plexus that drains into the internal iliac vein.

Uterine (fallopian) tubes

Each tube runs in the upper (free) border of the broad ligament and connects the uterine cavity with the peritoneal cavity in the region of the ovary.

The ovary

The ovary is the homologue of the testis in the male. One lies on each side of the uterus on the lateral pelvic wall, in a shallow depression called the ovarian fossa. It is attached to the back of the broad ligament by a peritoneal fold, the meso-ovarium.

  • Size about 4 x 2 x 1 cm and weight about 2 to 3.5 gm.
  • Grayish-pink in color.

Orientation

In the erect posture the long axis of the ovary is vertical.

  • Two ends: an upper (tubal) and a lower (uterine).
  • Two surfaces: lateral and medial.
  • Two borders: an anterior (mesovarian) and a posterior free border.

Blood supply

  • Arterial: the ovarian arteries, arising from the abdominal aorta at the level of the third lumbar vertebra.
  • Venous: the left ovarian vein drains into the left renal vein; the right ovarian vein drains into the inferior vena cava.

Female external genitalia

The female external genitalia (vulva or pudendum) consist of the vestibule of the vagina and its surrounding structures:

  • Mons pubis
  • Labia majora and labia minora
  • Clitoris
  • A pair of greater vestibular glands

Key Points / Quick Review

Uterus

  • Piriform, 7.5 x 5 x 2.5 cm, 30 to 40 gm, between bladder and rectum.
  • Parts: fundus, body, cervix; isthmus is the outer constriction between body and cervix.
  • Normally anteverted (about 90 degrees with the vagina) and anteflexed.
  • Utero-vesical and recto-vaginal (Douglas) pouches both contain small intestine loops.

Ligaments

  • Ligamentum transversale colli is the main support.
  • Round ligament: fundus, through the inguinal canal, to the labium majus.
  • Broad ligament parts: meso-salpinx, meso-ovarium, suspensory ligament of the ovary, mesometrium.

Blood supply

  • Uterine artery (internal iliac) anastomoses with the ovarian artery (aorta).
  • Uterine veins end in the internal iliac vein.
  • Vagina: vaginal artery and vaginal branch of the uterine artery.
  • Tube: medial 3/4 uterine artery, lateral 1/4 ovarian artery.
  • Ovarian artery arises at L3; left ovarian vein to left renal vein, right to the inferior vena cava.

Vagina, tubes, ovary, vulva

  • Vagina: 7.5 cm anterior wall, 9 cm posterior wall, four fornices; the posterior fornix is deepest and peritoneum-covered.
  • Tube parts: intramural (shortest), isthmus (narrowest), ampulla (widest, fertilization), infundibulum (fimbriae).
  • Ovary: 4 x 2 x 1 cm, grayish-pink, in the ovarian fossa, attached by the meso-ovarium.
  • Vulva: mons pubis, labia majora and minora, clitoris, vestibule, greater vestibular glands.
