Hypothalamo-hypophyseal system

The nervous and endocrine systems control all body functions, and the hypothalamo-hypophyseal connection is the most important link between them.

The hypothalamus is the nervous part and the pituitary (hypophysis) is the endocrine part. The hypothalamus reaches the pituitary by two routes.

Direct nervous connection

  • The hypothalamus secretes ADH and oxytocin
  • They pass through the hypothalamo-hypophysial nervous tract
  • They are stored in the posterior lobe, so called the neurohypophysis

Indirect vascular connection

  • The hypothalamus releases releasing and inhibiting factors
  • They reach the anterior pituitary through the hypothalamo-hypophysial portal circulation
  • They control anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis) hormones, which control most endocrine glands

The feedback loop and its types

In a typical loop, gland A liberates hormone A, which stimulates (+) gland B to liberate hormone B. Hormone B then regulates the liberation of hormone A.

Positive feedback loop

  • Hormone B stimulates hormone A
  • Hormone A then stimulates hormone B

Negative feedback loop

  • Hormone B inhibits hormone A
  • Hormone A then inhibits hormone B
High-yield

The purpose of feedback is to control the plasma level of hormone B.

Extrinsic negative feedback

Anterior pituitary TSH (thyrotropin) stimulates the thyroid gland to secrete thyroxine, which provides the standard example of negative feedback: increased thyroxine decreases TSH, while decreased thyroxine increases TSH.

Positive feedback

Positive feedback occurs when a hormone stimulates, directly or indirectly, its own production. It exists between the female sex hormones.

  • Direct long loop: increased estrogen from the ovary increases LH secretion from the anterior pituitary.
  • Indirect long loop: it takes place at the hypothalamic level, as shown below.
  • Auto positive (self-regulating): estrogen from a developing ovarian follicle increases the growth of that follicle directly, producing more estrogen.

Any positive feedback is intrinsically unstable: once the hormone reaches a critical level it can trigger a negative feedback mechanism that opposes further rise.

Intrinsic (intracellular) feedback

Intrinsic feedback is negative feedback at the cellular level. Two examples are given:

  • Wolff-Chaikoff effect: large quantities of iodide inside thyroid cells inhibit thyroid hormone synthesis and release.
  • Previously formed 25-hydroxycholecalciferol in liver cells inhibits the 25-hydroxylation of cholecalciferol.

Check yourself

Which hormones travel along the hypothalamo-hypophysial nervous tract?

ADH and oxytocin, which are stored in the posterior lobe.

How does long-loop negative feedback differ from short-loop?

Long-loop acts between the target gland and the anterior pituitary or hypothalamus; short-loop acts between the anterior pituitary and the hypothalamus.

Why is positive feedback intrinsically unstable?

At a critical hormone level it can trigger a negative feedback mechanism that opposes further rise.

Key Points / Quick Review

Hypothalamus and pituitary

  • Direct nervous connection: ADH and oxytocin stored in the posterior lobe.
  • Indirect vascular connection: releasing and inhibiting factors reach the anterior pituitary via the portal circulation.
  • Inhibitory factors: somatostatin and prolactin inhibitory factor.

Feedback basics

  • Feedback controls the plasma level of hormone B.
  • Negative: hormone B inhibits A. Positive: hormone B stimulates A.

Negative feedback types

  • Long-loop: target gland with anterior pituitary (direct) or hypothalamus (indirect).
  • Short-loop: anterior pituitary with hypothalamus.
  • Auto: a hormone on its own secreting cell.

Positive and intrinsic

  • Positive feedback is seen with estrogen and LH, and is intrinsically unstable.
  • Intrinsic feedback: Wolff-Chaikoff effect and inhibition of 25-hydroxylation.
