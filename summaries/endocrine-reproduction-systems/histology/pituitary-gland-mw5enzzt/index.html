Pineal gland (epiphysis cerebri)

Source: pages 19 to 20

Stroma

  • Capsule: the gland is enclosed by pia mater.
  • Trabeculae: very thin, descending from the capsule.
  • Reticular fibers: form a fine network.

Parenchyma

Pinealocytes

  • Arranged in cords and clumps separated by blood vessels
  • Large branched cells; large spherical or lobulated nucleus with prominent nucleolus; pale basophilic cytoplasm
  • EM: many mitochondria, small Golgi complex, RER, SER, secretory vesicles, many cytoplasmic processes
  • Secrete melatonin

Interstitial cells

  • Lie among the pinealocytes
  • Modified astrocytes (neuroglial cells)
  • Long cytoplasmic processes
High-yield

Brain sands (corpora arenacea) are extracellular calcified concretions. They increase in size and number with advancing age; their significance is unknown.

Pars distalis: structure and cell groups

In shortThe pars distalis is the largest part of the pituitary and contains cords of secretory cells split into chromophobes and chromophils.

Stroma and parenchyma

  • Very thin connective tissue capsule; delicate reticular fibers support the sinusoids and blood vessels.
  • Parenchyma: irregular cords or clusters of secretory endocrine cells with irregular blood sinusoids.

Classification by staining affinity

Chromophobes

  • About 50% of epithelial cells
  • Poor staining affinity: cytoplasm contains few granules
  • Reserve cells that can differentiate into chromophils

Chromophils

  • About 50% of epithelial cells
  • Intense staining affinity: abundant hormone in secretory granules
  • Accurate identification needs immunohistochemistry and electron microscopy
  • Divided into acidophils and basophils

Acidophils versus basophils

Acidophils

  • Large rounded cells, central rounded nucleus, acidophilic cytoplasm
  • PAS negative; orange G positive
  • Two types: somatotrophs and mammotrophs

Basophils

  • Polygonal cells, eccentric rounded nuclei, basophilic cytoplasm
  • PAS positive; orange G negative
  • Three types: thyrotrophs, corticotrophs, gonadotrophs
  • EM: large euchromatic nuclei with prominent nucleoli, few dilated rER cisternae, well-developed Golgi and mitochondria

Basophil hormones and their targets

Thyrotrophs: TSH (thyrotropin)
Stimulates the thyroid to release T3 and T4 into the bloodstream
Corticotrophs: ACTH
Acts on zona fasciculata and zona reticularis cells; stimulates glucocorticoid release in response to stress (anxiety, thirst, injury)
FSH in females
Stimulates growth of ovarian follicles
LH in females
Stimulates ovulation and development of the corpus luteum
FSH in males
Stimulates Sertoli cells, promoting spermatogenesis
ICSH in males
Stimulates testosterone production by the interstitial cells of the testis

Gonadotrophs secrete FSH, ICSH and LH. Corticotrophs also secrete gonadocorticoids.

Definition

Folliculo-stellate cells are non-secretory cells located between the secretory cells of the anterior pituitary. They form a communication network through gap junctions between their processes, transferring information throughout the gland.

Pars tuberalis and pars intermedia

Pars tuberalis

  • Upward extension of the anterior lobe
  • The most highly vascular part of the pituitary
  • Cells in cords or follicles near blood vessels
  • Basophilic cuboidal cells with few granules, glycogen and multiple lipid droplets
  • Function unknown; suggested to secrete FSH and LH

Pars intermedia

  • Not well developed in humans
  • Polygonal basophilic cells in cords or follicles with central faintly stained colloidal acidophilic material
  • Cytoplasm rich in mitochondria; small secretory granules (200 to 250 nm)
  • Amphibians and reptiles: cells secrete MSH, which stimulates melanin synthesis by melanocytes
  • Humans: MSH is secreted by some basophils of the pars distalis

Neurohypophysis

The neurohypophysis includes the median eminence, the infundibular stem and the pars nervosa.

Pars nervosa contents

Nerve fibers
Unmyelinated axons of secretory neurons in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus; they form the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract ending in the pars nervosa
Pituicytes
Highly branched cells with no secretory granules; supportive, like neuroglial cells
Herring's bodies
Accumulations of neurosecretory granules at axonal terminals in contact with blood capillaries; contain antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin) and oxytocin
Blood sinusoids
Network of wide fenestrated sinusoids

The hormones are made by secretory nerve cells in the supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei of the hypothalamus. The pituicytes only support them.

Microcirculation (hypophyseal portal circulation)

Source: pages 26 to 27

Superior hypophyseal arteries arise from the internal carotid and anastomose around the median eminence. They supply the hypothalamus, median eminence, infundibular stalk and pars distalis.

Superior hypophyseal arteries
Primary plexus (median eminence)
Portal veins
Secondary plexus (pars distalis sinusoids)

The venules connecting the primary to the secondary plexus constitute the hypophyseal portal circulation. This lets hypothalamic hormones reach the anterior pituitary directly to regulate its secretory cells.

  • Two inferior hypophyseal arteries, branches of the internal carotid, supply mainly the pars nervosa and to a lesser extent the pars distalis.
  • Venous blood from both lobes drains into the cavernous sinus through a number of venous channels.

Check yourself

Which cell group of the pars distalis is PAS positive and orange G negative?

The basophils (thyrotrophs, corticotrophs and gonadotrophs).

Where is the primary capillary plexus of the portal circulation located?

In the median eminence.

What do Herring's bodies contain?

Neurosecretory granules holding antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin) and oxytocin.

Key Points / Quick Review

Pineal gland

  • Capsule of pia mater; pinealocytes secrete melatonin
  • Interstitial cells are modified astrocytes
  • Brain sands are calcified concretions that increase with age

Pars distalis cells

  • Chromophobes about 50% (reserve cells); chromophils about 50%
  • Acidophils: somatotrophs and mammotrophs (PAS negative, orange G positive)
  • Basophils: thyrotrophs, corticotrophs, gonadotrophs (PAS positive, orange G negative)
  • Granule sizes: thyrotrophs 150 nm, somatotrophs 300 to 350 nm, gonadotrophs 200 to 400 nm, mammotrophs 500 to 900 nm

Other parts

  • Pars tuberalis: most vascular; function unknown
  • Pars intermedia: poorly developed in humans
  • Folliculo-stellate cells: non-secretory, linked by gap junctions

Neurohypophysis and circulation

  • Pars nervosa: unmyelinated axons from supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei, pituicytes, Herring's bodies, fenestrated sinusoids
  • Portal circulation: primary plexus in median eminence to secondary plexus in pars distalis
  • Inferior hypophyseal arteries supply mainly the pars nervosa; venous drainage is to the cavernous sinus
