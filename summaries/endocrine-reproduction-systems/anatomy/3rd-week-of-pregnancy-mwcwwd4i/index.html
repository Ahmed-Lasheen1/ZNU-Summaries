Gastrulation and the primitive streak

Gastrulation is the most characteristic event of the 3rd week.

Definition

Gastrulation is the process of formation of the three germ layers. It starts with formation of the primitive streak in the epiblast.

Formation of the primitive streak

On day 15, a longitudinal ridge appears in the midline at the caudal end of the dorsal aspect of the bilaminar embryonic disc, due to proliferation of epiblast cells. This ridge is the primitive streak.

At its cranial end is a rounded elevated area, the primitive node (Henson's node or knot), which surrounds a small primitive pit. As the embryonic disc elongates, the streak elongates along its central axis.

Roles of the primitive streak

  • Gives rise to the intraembryonic mesoderm.
  • Gives rise to the septum transversum.
  • Gives rise to the notochord.
  • Demarcates the embryo into left and right halves.

After gastrulation the streak and node diminish in size and normally disappear at the end of the 4th week.

Formation of the three germ layers

  1. Embryonic endoderm (day 15)

    Proliferating epiblast cells migrate through the primitive streak, invade and displace the hypoblast cells, and form the embryonic endoderm.

  2. Intraembryonic mesoderm (day 16)

    Epiblast cells migrate through the streak to lie between the epiblast and the embryonic endoderm, forming the third layer.

  3. Embryonic ectoderm

    The epiblast cells that remain after endoderm and intraembryonic mesoderm have formed become the embryonic ectoderm.

High-yield

All three embryonic germ layers arise from the epiblast.

The intraembryonic mesoderm separates the endoderm from the ectoderm everywhere except at three sites:

  • The prochordal plate (cranially).
  • The notochord.
  • The cloacal membrane (caudally).

The notochord

  1. Notochordal process

    It develops from the epiblast of the primitive node and grows cranially along the midline as a solid cellular cord. Its growth is limited by the oral (buccopharyngeal) membrane, the prochordal plate.

  2. Notochordal canal

    The opening of the primitive pit extends in the median plane and canalizes the notochordal plate. This canal is the neuro-enteric canal, which transports nutritive substance from the yolk sac to the amniotic cavity.

  3. Definitive notochord

    The roof and sides of the canal fold longitudinally to form the solid definitive notochord. The endoderm at the floor of the canal proliferates and closes, and the primitive pit also closes.

Functions of the notochord

  • Forms the central axis of the developing embryo (embryonic disc).
  • Induces formation of the neural tube from the overlying ectoderm.
  • Provides the central column around which the vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs develop.
High-yield

In later life the notochord disappears. Its remnants are the nucleus pulposus of the intervertebral discs and the apical ligament of the dens of the second cervical vertebra.

Neurulation

The process of formation of the neural tube is neurulation. The tube forms from the ectoderm overlying the notochord.

Neural tube
  • The neuroectodermal cells proliferate to form a thick plate, the neural plate.
  • The margins of the plate are elevated as neural folds, with the neural groove between them.
  • The groove deepens and the folds move toward the midline and fuse into a cylindrical neural tube.
  • The cells at the tips of the folds, the neural crests, do not take part in forming the tube. They separate and lie dorsolateral to it.

Subdivisions of the intraembryonic mesoderm

On day 17 a longitudinal groove in the midline divides the intraembryonic mesoderm into three parts.

Intraembryonic coelom

The lateral mesoderm is initially involved in forming the intraembryonic coelom. This U-shaped coelom develops from the fusion of many small cavities that appear in the lateral plate mesoderm. Later it gives rise to the serous membranes: pericardium, pleura and peritoneum.

Somites

At the end of the 3rd week the paraxial mesoderm lies on each side of the notochord and the developing neural tube. It segments into well-defined cubical blocks called somites, in a craniocaudal direction starting from the occipital region. Somites are arranged as pairs around the notochord.

Key terms

Check yourself

What does the primitive streak give rise to?

Intraembryonic mesoderm, septum transversum and notochord. It also demarcates the embryo into left and right halves.

At which three sites is the intraembryonic mesoderm absent?

The prochordal plate, the notochord and the cloacal membrane.

What does each part of a somite form?

The sclerotome forms vertebrae and ribs, the myotome forms skeletal muscles, and the dermatome forms the dermis of the skin.

Key Points / Quick Review

Gastrulation

  • Formation of the three germ layers, starting with the primitive streak (day 15).
  • Endoderm (day 15) and mesoderm (day 16) form from epiblast cells migrating through the streak.
  • The remaining epiblast becomes ectoderm.
  • The streak disappears at the end of the 4th week.

Notochord and neurulation

  • The notochord arises from the primitive node and induces the neural tube.
  • Remnants: nucleus pulposus and apical ligament of the dens.
  • Neural plate, folds and groove fuse into the neural tube; neural crests lie dorsolateral to it.

Mesoderm and somites

  • Day 17: paraxial, intermediate and lateral mesoderm.
  • Paraxial gives somites, intermediate gives the urogenital system, lateral gives body wall and cavities.
  • First somite pair on day 20, then 3 pairs daily; 42 to 44 by the end of the 5th week.
  • Sclerotome: vertebrae and ribs; myotome: muscles; dermatome: dermis.
