Hyperglycemia and diabetes mellitus

Hyperglycemia means a fasting blood glucose level above 126 mg/dl. It has several causes, including diabetes mellitus, thyrotoxicosis, Cushing disease, bronzed diabetes (iron toxicity), stress diabetes, and drugs such as corticosteroids.

Diabetes mellitus is a metabolic disturbance characterized by a fasting glucose level above 126 mg/dl. It results from insufficient insulin release or insulin resistance, in association with excess glucagon release.

Types of diabetes mellitus

Type 1 (insulin dependent)

  • Age of onset: during childhood
  • Nutritional state: thin or normal
  • Prevalence: 10 to 20%
  • Genetic predisposition: moderate
  • Cause: destruction of beta cells
  • Ketosis: common
  • Plasma insulin: low or absent
  • Treatment: insulin injection

Type 2 (non-insulin dependent)

  • Age of onset: after age 35
  • Nutritional state: often obese
  • Prevalence: 80 to 90%
  • Genetic predisposition: very strong
  • Cause: insulin deficiency and insulin resistance
  • Ketosis: less common
  • Plasma insulin: normal, decreased or high
  • Treatment: oral hypoglycemic drugs

Triggers of type 1 diabetes

Environmental factors trigger the initial development of type 1 diabetes. These include viral infection, vaccines, low levels of vitamin D, cow's milk, and increased insulin demand.

Diagnosis (ADA 2016)

The American Diabetes Association 2016 guideline gives four options for diagnosing diabetes:

  • Fasting blood glucose more than 126 mg/dl (7 mmol/l)
  • Two-hour postprandial glucose more than 200 mg/dl (11.1 mmol/l)
  • HbA1c more than 6.5%
  • Random blood glucose more than 200 mg/dl

Biochemical disturbances

Insulin deficiency affects carbohydrate, protein and lipid metabolism.

Carbohydrate metabolism

Glucose uptake and oxidation fall while gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis rise. This causes:

  • Low intracellular glucose, with hunger and polyphagia
  • Hyperglycemia, which raises plasma osmolality and causes dehydration, thirst and polydipsia
  • Dehydration of brain cells, which can lead to hyperglycemic hyperosmolar coma
  • Glucosuria once blood glucose exceeds the renal threshold of 180 mg/dl
High-yield

Glucose in the urine causes osmotic diuresis, leading to polyuria and the loss of water-soluble vitamins such as B1, and of sodium and potassium.

Protein metabolism

  • Increased protein breakdown and gluconeogenesis, with phosphate release that causes hyperphosphatemia
  • Muscle wasting from excessive breakdown of tissue protein
  • Decreased antibody formation, causing low resistance and infection
  • Delayed wound healing

Lipid metabolism

  • Excessive lipolysis, with free fatty acids and glycerol mobilized to the blood, causing weight loss
  • Hyperlipidemia, which may cause atherosclerosis
  • Fatty liver
  • Excessive ketone body formation, causing ketonemia and ketosis, which can lead to diabetic ketoacidosis

Acute complications

  1. Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
  2. Hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state (HHS)
  3. Hypoglycemia

Hypoglycemia

Hypoglycemia means a blood glucose level of 45 mg/dl or less. It is divided into fasting and reactive types.

Hypoglycemia
  • Fasting occurs at any time, even during fasting
    • Pancreatic disease insulinoma
    • Liver disease less glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis
    • Adrenocortical disease less glucocorticoid secretion
  • Reactive triggered by a stimulus, usually after a meal
    • Drugs and poisons insulin or oral hypoglycemic overdose, liver poisons
    • Postgastrectomy rapid glucose absorption, excess insulin
    • Inborn error hereditary fructose intolerance

Glucosuria

Glucosuria is the presence of detectable amounts of glucose in the urine. It is either hyperglycemic, when blood glucose rises above the renal threshold, or normoglycemic, when blood glucose is normal but renal tubular reabsorption is reduced.

Glucosuria
  • Hyperglycemic
    • Diabetes mellitus and bronzed diabetes
    • Adrenaline glucosuria emotion, stress, pheochromocytoma
    • Excess diabetogenic hormones growth hormone, glucocorticoids, thyroid
  • Normoglycemic (renal)
    • Diabetes innocens congenitally low renal threshold
    • Pregnancy glucosuria seen in about 20% of normal pregnant women
    • Renal disease nephritis, nephrosis, advanced tubular disease

Check yourself

What is the renal threshold for glucose?

180 mg/dl. Above this, glucose is excreted in the urine.

Why does pregnancy glucosuria occur in normal blood glucose?

Glomerular filtration rate increases by about 50% during pregnancy.

Which acute complication results from brain cell dehydration?

Hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state (hyperglycemic hyperosmolar coma).

Key Points / Quick Review

Definitions and thresholds

  • Hyperglycemia: fasting glucose more than 126 mg/dl
  • Hypoglycemia: 45 mg/dl or less
  • Renal threshold for glucose: 180 mg/dl

Types

  • Type 1: childhood onset, beta-cell destruction, insulin injection
  • Type 2: after age 35, obesity, insulin resistance, oral drugs

Complications

  • Acute: DKA, hyperglycemic hyperosmolar state, hypoglycemia
  • Lipid: hyperlipidemia, fatty liver, ketosis
  • Protein: muscle wasting, hyperphosphatemia, impaired healing
