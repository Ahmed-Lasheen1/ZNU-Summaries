Purpose and respiratory basis

Source: page 84

These therapies deliver medication directly to the lungs to improve respiratory function in acute and chronic respiratory diseases.

  • Treatment is targeted directly where it is needed.
  • Benefits: improved medication efficacy and fewer systemic side effects.

Respiratory anatomy

  • Upper respiratory tract: nasal cavity, pharynx and larynx.
  • Lower respiratory tract: trachea, bronchi and bronchioles.
  • Alveoli: tiny air sacs where gas exchange occurs.

Mechanisms of gas exchange

Ventilation
Movement of air in and out of the lungs
Diffusion
Movement of oxygen and carbon dioxide across the alveolar-capillary membrane
Perfusion
Flow of blood through the pulmonary capillaries, facilitating gas exchange

Indications

Source: page 85

Common indications

Asthma
Relieve acute bronchospasm and manage chronic symptoms
COPD
Improve airflow and reduce symptoms
Cystic fibrosis
Help clear thick mucus from the airways
Acute respiratory infections
Reduce inflammation and facilitate mucus clearance

Other specific conditions treated

Bronchitis
Reduce airway inflammation and improve breathing
Allergic rhinitis
Alleviate symptoms by reducing airway inflammation
Pneumonia
Assist medication delivery in severe cases

Devices fall into three groups: nebulizers, metered-dose inhalers (MDIs) and dry powder inhalers (DPIs).

Nebulization therapy

Source: page 89
Definition

Nebulization converts liquid medication into a mist that can be inhaled deeply into the lungs, delivering the drug directly to the airways and lungs.

Procedure and technique

  1. Preparation

    Assemble the nebulizer and ensure all parts are clean.

  2. Medication

    Measure and add the prescribed medication to the nebulizer cup.

  3. Positioning

    Place the mask or mouthpiece on the patient.

  4. Nebulization

    Turn on the nebulizer; the patient breathes normally through the mouthpiece.

  5. Completion

    Ensure the therapy is complete, then clean the equipment.

Medication types used

Bronchodilators (e.g. albuterol)
Relax airway muscles
Corticosteroids (e.g. fluticasone)
Reduce inflammation
Mucolytics (e.g. acetylcysteine)
Break down mucus

Inhalation therapy: MDIs and DPIs

Source: page 90

MDI technique

An MDI delivers medication via a pressurized canister that produces a fine aerosol spray.

  1. Shake the inhaler

    This mixes the medication.

  2. Prime if necessary

    Follow the manufacturer's instructions.

  3. Inhale

    Breathe out fully, place the mouthpiece in the mouth, and press the canister while inhaling deeply and slowly.

  4. Hold breath

    Hold for 10 seconds to allow the medication to reach the lungs.

  5. Rinse mouth

    This prevents side effects such as oral thrush.

DPI technique

A DPI delivers medication as a dry powder that is inhaled directly into the lungs.

  1. Load the dose

    Follow the device-specific instructions.

  2. Inhale

    Breathe out fully, place the mouthpiece in the mouth, and inhale forcefully and deeply.

  3. Hold breath

    Hold for 10 seconds to ensure the medication reaches the lungs.

  4. Rinse mouth

    This prevents local side effects.

Don't confuse

MDI: inhale deeply and slowly while pressing the canister. DPI: inhale forcefully and deeply, with no actuation to coordinate.

Complications and management

Source: pages 90 to 91

Nebulization therapy

  • Complication: local irritation (throat dryness, coughing)
  • Complication: infection if the nebulizer is not properly cleaned
  • Prevention: regularly clean and disinfect the nebulizer
  • Prevention: hydrate; encourage fluid intake to minimize throat dryness

Inhalation therapy

  • Complication: oral thrush, especially with corticosteroids
  • Complication: dry mouth, common with some inhaled medications
  • Prevention: rinse the mouth after using MDIs or DPIs
  • Prevention: check technique to maximize medication delivery

Patient education, documentation and follow-up

Source: pages 91 to 92

Instructing patients on device use

  • Demonstrate technique: step-by-step demonstration of device use.
  • Use visual aids: diagrams or videos.
  • Provide written instructions for reference.

Ensuring compliance and correct usage

  • Regular follow-up to review technique and medication adherence.
  • Encourage questions about the therapy.
  • Monitor effectiveness: track symptoms and adjust therapy as needed.

Documentation

  • Medication details: type, dose and frequency.
  • Patient response: any adverse reactions or side effects.
  • Device used: type of device and any issues encountered.

Follow-up care

  • Monitor symptoms: assess symptoms and response to therapy at follow-up visits.
  • Adjust therapy: modify the plan based on patient feedback and clinical evaluation.
  • Reinforce education: continuously reinforce proper use and technique.

Check yourself

Source: pages 84 to 92
Which nebulizer type is useful for medications that are sensitive to heat?

The ultrasonic nebulizer, which uses high-frequency sound waves.

How does the inhalation effort differ between an MDI and a DPI?

An MDI needs slow, deep inhalation coordinated with pressing the canister; a DPI needs a forceful, deep inhalation with no coordination.

Why should the mouth be rinsed after using an MDI or DPI?

To prevent side effects such as oral thrush, especially with corticosteroids.

Key Points / Quick Review

Purpose and indications

  • Deliver medication directly to the lungs with fewer systemic side effects
  • Asthma, COPD, cystic fibrosis, acute respiratory infections, bronchitis, allergic rhinitis, pneumonia

Devices

  • Jet: compressed air; ultrasonic: sound waves (heat-sensitive drugs); mesh: vibrating mesh (portable, costly)
  • MDI needs coordination; DPI needs forceful inhalation

Medications

  • Bronchodilators relax airway muscles; corticosteroids reduce inflammation; mucolytics break down mucus

Technique and safety

  • Hold breath 10 seconds after MDI or DPI; rinse mouth afterwards
  • Clean the nebulizer; hydrate to reduce throat dryness

Education and follow-up

  • Demonstrate, use visual aids, give written instructions
  • Document medication, patient response and device; adjust therapy and reinforce education
