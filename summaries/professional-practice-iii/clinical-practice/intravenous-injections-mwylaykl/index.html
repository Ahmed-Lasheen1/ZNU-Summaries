Definition and purpose

Source: page 62
Definition

An intravenous (IV) injection is a procedure where medication or fluids are administered directly into a vein.

This gives rapid absorption and distribution, which is essential for immediate therapeutic effects, precise dosage control, and when oral administration is not feasible.

Purposes

  • Immediate effect: the medication enters the bloodstream directly and acts quickly.
  • Controlled dosage: precise control over the amount and rate of administration.
  • Fluid resuscitation: essential for managing severe dehydration or blood loss.

Types of IV injections

Source: pages 62 to 63

Bolus injection

  • A single, rapid injection of medication into a vein
  • For medications needing immediate action, such as epinephrine in anaphylaxis
  • Given over a short period, often within a few minutes

Infusion

  • Continuous or intermittent administration over an extended period
  • For slow, sustained delivery, such as antibiotics over several hours
  • Uses an infusion drip with a controlled flow rate

Push dose medication

  • A concentrated dose pushed quickly into the vein
  • For emergencies where a rapid effect is needed
  • Given with a syringe, often within advanced resuscitation protocols

Common medications administered

Source: page 63
Analgesics
Pain relievers, such as morphine or fentanyl
Antibiotics
For infections, such as vancomycin or ceftriaxone
Electrolytes
Correct imbalances: potassium chloride, calcium gluconate
Fluids
Hydration: normal saline or dextrose solutions

Preparation

Source: pages 64 to 65

Patient and medication verification

  • Identification: confirm patient identity using two identifiers (for example name and date of birth).
  • Medication orders: check the order for the correct drug, dosage and administration route.

Equipment

  • IV catheter: size appropriate to the patient’s vein and the medication.
  • Syringes and needles: sterile and the correct size for the procedure.
  • Antiseptic wipes: for site preparation.
  • Adhesive dressings: to secure the catheter in place.

Preparation steps

  1. Hand hygiene

    Wash hands thoroughly.

  2. Prepare equipment

    Assemble the IV catheter, syringe, medication and sterile gloves.

  3. Prepare the medication

    Draw it up into the syringe, ensuring no air bubbles are present.

Technique

Source: pages 66 to 67

Vein selection

  • Choose a large, visible vein, for example the median cubital vein.
  • Avoid veins that are inflamed or hard.

Aseptic technique

  • Site cleaning: antiseptic wipes in a circular motion, from the center moving outward.
  • Gloving: wear sterile gloves to maintain aseptic conditions.

Insertion

  1. Apply tourniquet

    Engorges the veins and makes them more prominent.

  2. Insert the needle

    At a 15–30-degree angle with the bevel up. Once blood is aspirated, advance the catheter into the vein.

  3. Confirm placement

    Aspirate blood into the catheter.

  4. Secure the catheter

    Remove the needle while holding the catheter in place, then secure with adhesive tape and a sterile dressing.

Medication administration

  • Bolus injection: push the medication into the vein rapidly, as prescribed.
  • Infusion: connect the IV line to the catheter and set the infusion rate according to the physician’s order.

Aftercare

Source: page 67

Site monitoring

  • Check for redness, swelling or discomfort at the insertion site.
  • Observe the patient for any immediate reactions to the medication.

Documentation

  • Record the medication administered, dose, time, and any observations or complications.

Disposal

  • Dispose of used needles and sharps in a designated sharps container.
  • Ensure the area and equipment are properly cleaned and disposed of.

Case studies

Source: page 68

Emergency bolus injection

  • Scenario: a patient arrives with severe anaphylaxis
  • Medication: epinephrine IV bolus as prescribed
  • Technique: quick venipuncture, then inject rapidly
  • Monitoring: observe for improvement and adverse reactions

Continuous infusion

  • Scenario: a patient needs a 24-hour antibiotic infusion
  • Medication: prepare and set up the prescribed antibiotic infusion
  • Technique: insert a peripheral catheter, connect the infusion set, adjust the flow rate
  • Monitoring: check the site regularly and confirm delivery as planned

Key Points / Quick Review

Definition and types

  • IV injection delivers medication or fluids directly into a vein
  • Purposes: immediate effect, controlled dosage, fluid resuscitation
  • Types: bolus injection, infusion, push dose medication

Medications and risks

  • Analgesics, antibiotics, electrolytes and fluids
  • Phlebitis: sterile techniques and site care
  • Extravasation: proper placement and site monitoring
  • Air embolism: remove air from syringes and lines
  • Thrombosis: change infusion sites regularly

Preparation and technique

  • Two identifiers; check drug, dosage and route
  • Large visible vein, such as the median cubital; avoid inflamed or hard veins
  • Clean in circular motion from the center outward; wear sterile gloves
  • Tourniquet, 15–30 degrees with bevel up, advance catheter, confirm, secure

Aftercare

  • Monitor the site and the patient’s reactions
  • Document medication, dose, time and complications
  • Dispose of sharps in a designated container
