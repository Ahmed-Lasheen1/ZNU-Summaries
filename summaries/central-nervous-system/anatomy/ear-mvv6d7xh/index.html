External ear

The ear has three anatomical parts: the external ear, the middle ear and the inner ear. The external ear consists of the auricle and the external acoustic meatus.

Auricle

  • Consists of the lobule and the pinna.
  • Nerve supply: auriculotemporal, lesser occipital and great auricular nerves.

External acoustic meatus

  • Connects the auricle with the tympanic membrane.
  • An S-shaped tube, 25 mm long, not straight.
  • Lateral 1/3 is cartilaginous; medial 2/3 is bony.
  • The tympanic membrane forms an angle of 55 degrees with the floor of the meatus.

Outer part

  • Directed upwards
  • Directed backwards
  • Directed medially

Inner part

  • Directed downwards
  • Directed forwards
  • Directed medially

Nerve supply and lymphatics of the meatus

  • Nerves: auriculotemporal nerve, auricular branch of the vagus and auricular branch of the facial nerve.
  • Lymph from the external ear drains to the parotid, posterior auricular and superficial cervical lymph nodes.

Tympanic membrane

A circular, thin and delicate membrane lying at the end of the external acoustic meatus.

  • Diameter is about 8 to 10 millimeters.
  • Concave laterally: when lit by an otoscope, the concavity produces a cone of light radiating downwards and forwards.
  • Its circumference is thickened into an annular ring, attached to a circular groove in the temporal bone, the tympanic sulcus.
  • The sulcus is deficient above, forming the tympanic notch.
  • The handle of the malleus is enclosed between the fibrous and mucous layers of the membrane.
  • Anterior and posterior malleolar folds pass from the lateral process of the malleus to the anterior and posterior margins of the tympanic notch.

Pars flaccida

  • Lies between and above the two malleolar folds
  • Triangular, thin and flaccid

Pars tensa

  • The rest of the membrane
  • Thick and tense

Nerve supply

The membrane has a rich sensory supply (5th, 9th and 10th cranial nerves) and is extremely sensitive to pain.

  • Outer surface: auriculotemporal nerve and auricular branch of the vagus.
  • Inner surface: tympanic branch of the glossopharyngeal nerve.
Clinical relevance

Direct trauma to the membrane, as in ear wash, may lead to fainting attacks due to stimulation of the auricular branch of the vagus.

The middle ear is a six-walled bony chamber in the petrous part of the temporal bone.

Middle ear: contents and muscles

The middle ear contains:

  • Three ossicles: malleus, incus and stapes.
  • Two muscles: tensor tympani and stapedius.
  • Two nerves: the chorda tympani, and the tympanic branch of the glossopharyngeal nerve with the tympanic plexus.
  • Vessels of the tympanic cavity.

Tensor tympani

  • Function: tenses the tympanic membrane
  • Nerve supply: mandibular nerve

Stapedius

  • Function: draws the stapes laterally
  • Nerve supply: facial nerve
High-yield

Together the two muscles lessen the transmission of sound vibrations, a protective mechanism against sounds of higher intensity.

Branch of the middle meningeal artery
Inferior tympanic artery
Branch of the ascending pharyngeal artery

Veins, nerves and lymph

  • Veins drain into the pterygoid venous plexus and the superior petrosal sinus.
  • Nerve supply is the tympanic plexus, formed by the tympanic branch of the glossopharyngeal nerve and the superior and inferior caroticotympanic nerves, which arise from the sympathetic plexus around the internal carotid artery.
  • The middle ear and mastoid antrum drain into the preauricular and upper deep cervical lymph nodes.
  • Auditory tube: the medial part drains into the upper deep cervical nodes; the lateral part drains into the lymphatics of the middle ear.

Inner ear (labyrinth)

The bony labyrinth is a group of connected cavities medial to the middle ear within the petrous temporal bone. They contain a clear fluid, perilymph, in which the membranous labyrinth sinks.

Cochlea

Has two and a half spiral turns and presents the round window, closed by the secondary tympanic membrane.

Vestibule

A small ovoid cavity containing the utricle and saccule of the membranous labyrinth. It receives:

Anteriorly
The cochlea
Posteriorly
The five openings of the semicircular canals
Laterally
The oval window, closed by the footplate (base) of the stapes
Medially
Opening of the vestibular aqueduct and passage of vestibular and cochlear nerve fibers

Semicircular canals

Each canal is about two thirds of the circumference of a circle.

Vestibulocochlear nerve

A pure sensory nerve carrying special somatic afferent fibers from the inner ear.

Vestibular division

  • Equilibrium
  • Ends in the vestibular nuclei

Cochlear division

  • Hearing
  • Ends in the cochlear nuclei

The nerve originates at the cochlea, the semicircular canals, the maculae, the saccule and the utricle.

Vestibular and cochlear nuclei
  • The vestibular nuclei are located in the vestibular area in the lateral part of the floor of the 4th ventricle (equilibrium).
  • The nuclei are described as lying in the medulla, related to the inferior cerebellar peduncle.

Key terms

Check yourself

What are the composition and length of the external acoustic meatus?

It is an S-shaped tube 25 mm long: the lateral 1/3 is cartilaginous and the medial 2/3 is bony.

Which nerves supply the outer and inner surfaces of the tympanic membrane?

The outer surface is supplied by the auriculotemporal nerve and the auricular branch of the vagus; the inner surface by the tympanic branch of the glossopharyngeal nerve.

Which structures lie in the anterior (carotid) wall of the middle ear?

A thin plate of bone separating the cavity from the internal carotid artery, with the canal for tensor tympani above and the canal for the auditory tube below.

Key Points / Quick Review

External ear

  • Auricle (lobule and pinna) supplied by auriculotemporal, lesser occipital and great auricular nerves.
  • Meatus: S-shaped, 25 mm, lateral 1/3 cartilaginous and medial 2/3 bony.
  • Meatus nerves: auriculotemporal, auricular branches of vagus and facial.

Tympanic membrane

  • 8 to 10 mm diameter; pars flaccida above the malleolar folds, pars tensa the rest.
  • Outer surface: auriculotemporal and vagus; inner surface: glossopharyngeal.
  • Ear wash trauma may cause fainting via the auricular branch of the vagus.

Middle ear

  • Six walls: lateral, roof, floor, anterior, posterior and medial.
  • Contains 3 ossicles, 2 muscles, chorda tympani and the tympanic plexus.
  • Tensor tympani (mandibular nerve) and stapedius (facial nerve) lessen sound transmission.
  • Four tympanic arteries; veins drain to the pterygoid plexus and superior petrosal sinus.

Inner ear and nerve

  • Bony labyrinth: cochlea, vestibule and three semicircular canals, filled with perilymph.
  • Vestibulocochlear nerve is pure sensory: vestibular for equilibrium, cochlear for hearing.
  • It passes through the internal acoustic meatus and enters the brainstem at the pontomedullary junction.
