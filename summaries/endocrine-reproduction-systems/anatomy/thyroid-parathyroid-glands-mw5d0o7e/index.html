Thyroid gland: position and shape

The thyroid gland is the largest endocrine gland of the body and secretes T3, T4 (thyroxine) and calcitonin.

High-yield

It is the only endocrine gland located superficially in the body.

Position and size

  • Lies in the lower front and sides of the neck, opposite C5, C6, C7 and T1.
  • Weighs about 25 gm. Each lobe is 5 cm long, 3 cm wide and 2 cm thick.
  • The isthmus measures about 1/2 inch long and 1/2 inch wide.

Parts and extension

  • H-shaped: conical right and left lateral lobes joined by an isthmus.
  • Each lobe extends up to the oblique line of the thyroid cartilage and down to the 5th tracheal ring.
  • The isthmus lies across the midline in front of the 2nd, 3rd and 4th tracheal rings.
  • An occasional pyramidal lobe projects up from the isthmus, usually left of the midline. It may be tied to the hyoid body by a fibromuscular band, the levator glandulae thyroideae.

Capsules of the thyroid

The gland is invested by an inner and an outer capsule.

True capsule (inner)

  • Peripheral condensation of the gland's own connective tissue.
  • A dense venous plexus lies deep to it.

False capsule (outer)

  • Derived from splitting of the pretracheal fascia.
  • Thickens on the medial surface of the lobe as the suspensory ligament of Berry, joining the lobe to the cricoid cartilage so the gland moves with swallowing.
Clinical relevance

To avoid hemorrhage from the venous plexus, the gland is removed together with the true capsule in thyroidectomy.

Relations of the lobe and isthmus

Each lobe is roughly conical, with an apex, a base, three surfaces (lateral, medial, posterolateral) and two borders (anterior and posterior).

Thyroid lobe

Apex
Directed upward to the oblique line of the thyroid cartilage
Base
Reaches the 5th or 6th tracheal ring; related to the inferior thyroid artery and recurrent laryngeal nerve
Lateral surface
Convex; covered by sternothyroid, sternohyoid and superior belly of omohyoid; sternocleidomastoid overlaps it inferiorly
Posterolateral surface
Carotid sheath (common carotid artery, internal jugular vein, vagus); ansa cervicalis in its anterior wall; cervical sympathetic chain behind it
Anterior border
Thin; separates the superficial and medial surfaces
Posterior border
Thick; separates the medial and posterior surfaces; related to the arterial anastomosis between superior and inferior thyroid arteries and to the parathyroid glands

Medial surface of the lobe

  • Two tubes: trachea and esophagus.
  • Two muscles: inferior constrictor and cricothyroid.
  • Two cartilages: cricoid and thyroid.
  • Two nerves: external and recurrent laryngeal nerves.

Isthmus

Anterior surface
Sternohyoid, sternothyroid and anterior jugular veins
Posterior surface
2nd, 3rd and 4th tracheal rings
Superior border
Anastomosis between the anterior branches of the two superior thyroid arteries
Inferior border
Inferior thyroid vein and thyroidea ima artery

Blood supply, lymph and nerves

The gland is highly vascular.

Arteries

Superior thyroid
Branch of the external carotid artery
Inferior thyroid
Branch of the thyrocervical trunk (first part of subclavian artery)
Thyroidea ima (30% of cases)
Branch of the brachiocephalic trunk, or directly from the aortic arch

Veins

Superior thyroid vein
Internal jugular vein
Middle thyroid vein
Internal jugular vein
Inferior thyroid veins
Left brachiocephalic vein

Lymphatics

Two groups follow the arteries: the upper group (with the superior thyroid artery) drains to prelaryngeal and upper deep cervical (jugulodigastric) nodes; the lower group (with the inferior thyroid artery) drains to pretracheal and lower deep cervical (juguloomohyoid) nodes.

Nerve supply

  • Parasympathetic: from the vagus nerve.
  • Sympathetic: from the superior, middle and inferior cervical ganglia, mainly the middle one.

Surgical anatomy: ligating the thyroid arteries

Clinical relevance
  • Near the apex the superior thyroid artery lies superficial and the external laryngeal nerve lies deep. Ligate the artery as close to the apex as possible to spare the nerve.
  • The recurrent laryngeal nerve lies very close to the inferior thyroid artery near the base. Ligate the artery as far from the base as possible to spare the nerve.

Parathyroid glands

There are two pairs (superior and inferior) of small, oval, yellowish-brown glands about 5 mm in diameter, which secrete parathormone.

  • They lie along the posterior borders of the thyroid lobes, within the thyroid capsule.

Superior parathyroid

  • At the middle of the posterior border of the lobe.
  • From the 4th pharyngeal pouch (parathyroid-IV).

Inferior parathyroid

  • On the posterior border near the lower pole.
  • From the 3rd pharyngeal pouch (parathyroid-III).
  • Arterial supply: chiefly the inferior thyroid artery, with collateral supply from the superior thyroid and thyroidea ima arteries.
  • Venous drainage: into the superior, middle and inferior thyroid veins.

Check yourself

Which structure makes the thyroid move with swallowing?

The suspensory ligament of Berry, a thickening of the false capsule that joins the lobe to the cricoid cartilage.

Where do the superior and middle thyroid veins drain?

Into the internal jugular vein.

Which pharyngeal pouch gives rise to the inferior parathyroid gland?

The 3rd pouch (parathyroid-III).

Key Points / Quick Review

Thyroid gland

  • Largest, only superficial endocrine gland; opposite C5, C6, C7, T1; about 25 gm.
  • H-shaped: two lobes plus an isthmus over tracheal rings 2 to 4; occasional pyramidal lobe.
  • Secretes T3, T4 and calcitonin.

Capsules

  • True capsule (own connective tissue) with a venous plexus deep to it; removed with the gland.
  • False capsule from pretracheal fascia; ligament of Berry joins lobe to cricoid.

Supply and drainage

  • Arteries: superior (external carotid), inferior (thyrocervical trunk), ima (30%).
  • Veins: superior and middle to internal jugular; inferior to left brachiocephalic.
  • Lymph: prelaryngeal/upper deep cervical and pretracheal/lower deep cervical.

Surgery

  • Superior thyroid artery: ligate close to apex (external laryngeal nerve).
  • Inferior thyroid artery: ligate away from base (recurrent laryngeal nerve).

Parathyroids

  • Two pairs, about 5 mm, on the posterior borders within the thyroid capsule; secrete parathormone.
  • Superior from 4th pouch; inferior from 3rd pouch; supplied chiefly by the inferior thyroid artery.
