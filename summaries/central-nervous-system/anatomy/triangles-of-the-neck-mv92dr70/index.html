Suprahyoid muscles

Four muscles lie above the hyoid bone: digastric, stylohyoid, mylohyoid, and geniohyoid.

The mylohyoid raphe is a fibrous band running in the median plane from the symphysis menti to the hyoid bone.

Nerve supply

All infrahyoid muscles are supplied by the ansa cervicalis (C1, 2, 3) except thyrohyoid, which is supplied by fibers from C1 through the hypoglossal nerve.

Ansa cervicalis

Two roots unite in front of the carotid sheath to form the ansa cervicalis:

  • Descendens hypoglossi: C1 fibers that pass with the hypoglossal nerve.
  • Descendens cervicalis: fibers from the ventral rami of C2 and C3 (cervical plexus), descending in front of the internal jugular vein.

It gives a branch to each of the superior belly of omohyoid, inferior belly of omohyoid, sternohyoid, and sternothyroid.

Anterolateral neck and sternomastoid

The anterolateral neck is a quadrangular space bounded anteriorly by the median plane, posteriorly by the anterior border of trapezius, above by the base of the mandible, and below by the clavicle. The sternomastoid lies obliquely across it and divides it into the anterior and posterior triangles.

Sternomastoid

  • Origin: sternal head from the anterior surface of the manubrium sterni; clavicular head from the upper surface of the medial third of the clavicle.
  • Insertion: lateral surface of the mastoid process.
  • Nerve supply: motor by the spinal root of the accessory nerve; proprioceptive by fibers from the ventral rami of C2 and C3.
  • Action: flexes the cervical vertebral column; laterally flexes the neck, bringing the ear toward the shoulder of the same side and turning the face to the opposite side.
Clinical relevance
  • Being thick and strong, sternomastoid protects the underlying structures, especially in suicidal attempts.
  • Torticollis (congenital or spasmodic): the muscle on one side is shorter, drawing the mastoid process and auricle toward the shoulder of the same side and turning the face to the opposite side.

Posterior triangle

Boundaries

  • Anteriorly: posterior border of sternomastoid.
  • Posteriorly: anterior border of trapezius.
  • Base: middle third of the clavicle.
  • Apex: where sternomastoid and trapezius meet at the mastoid process.

The inferior belly of omohyoid divides it into the occipital triangle (above) and the supraclavicular triangle (below).

Contents

Nerves

  • Cutaneous branches of the cervical plexus, piercing the deep fascia at the middle of the posterior border of sternomastoid:
    • Great auricular: scalp behind the auricle.
    • Lesser occipital: scalp behind the auricle.
    • Transverse (cervical) cutaneous nerve of the neck: skin of the anterior neck.
    • Supraclavicular: crosses the corresponding thirds of the clavicle to the skin of the pectoral region.
  • Spinal root of the accessory nerve, supplying sternomastoid and trapezius.
  • Trunks of the brachial plexus.

Veins

The subclavian vein (at the base), and the external jugular vein and its tributaries.

Arteries

  • Third part of the subclavian artery.
  • Suprascapular and transverse cervical arteries, branches of the thyrocervical trunk (from the 1st part of the subclavian artery).
  • Occipital artery (from the external carotid), crossing the apex.

Anterior triangle

Boundaries and subdivisions

  • Anteriorly: median plane of the neck.
  • Posteriorly: anterior border of sternomastoid.
  • Superiorly: base of the mandible.

The anterior belly of digastric, the posterior belly of digastric, and the superior belly of omohyoid subdivide it into four triangles: submental (half on each side), muscular, carotid, and digastric (submandibular region).

Check yourself

Which nerve supplies the anterior belly of digastric, and which supplies the posterior belly?

The anterior belly is supplied by the nerve to mylohyoid; the posterior belly by a branch of the facial nerve.

Which infrahyoid muscle is not supplied by the ansa cervicalis?

Thyrohyoid, which is supplied by C1 fibers through the hypoglossal nerve.

What divides the posterior triangle into the occipital and supraclavicular triangles?

The inferior belly of omohyoid: the occipital triangle lies above it and the supraclavicular triangle below it.

Key Points / Quick Review

Suprahyoid muscles

  • Digastric, stylohyoid, mylohyoid, geniohyoid.
  • Nerve to mylohyoid: mylohyoid and anterior belly of digastric.
  • Facial nerve: stylohyoid and posterior belly of digastric.
  • Geniohyoid: C1 via hypoglossal nerve.

Infrahyoid muscles

  • Sternohyoid, omohyoid, sternothyroid, thyrohyoid.
  • Ansa cervicalis (C1, 2, 3) supplies all except thyrohyoid.
  • Sternothyroid depresses the larynx; thyrohyoid elevates it.

Sternomastoid

  • Motor: spinal accessory; proprioception: C2, C3.
  • Turns the face to the opposite side; shortening causes torticollis.

Posterior triangle

  • Sternomastoid, trapezius, middle third of clavicle.
  • Occipital (above) and supraclavicular (below) omohyoid.
  • Cervical plexus cutaneous nerves, spinal accessory nerve, brachial plexus trunks, subclavian and external jugular veins.

Anterior triangle

  • Submental, muscular, carotid, and digastric triangles.
  • Carotid triangle: carotid sheath, external carotid artery, spinal accessory root, ansa cervicalis, hypoglossal nerve.
