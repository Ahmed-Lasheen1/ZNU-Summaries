Definition, shape and boundaries

Source: page 47
Definition

The perineum is the tissue between the thighs that closes the pelvic outlet and lies below the pelvic diaphragm (levator ani muscles).

It is diamond in shape, with 4 angles and 4 boundaries.

Anteriorly
Symphysis pubis
Laterally
Pubic arches and sacrotuberous ligaments
Posteriorly
Coccyx

Divisions of the perineum

Source: page 47

An imaginary transverse line connecting the two ischial tuberosities divides the perineum into an anterior urogenital triangle and a posterior anal triangle.

This line passes across the perineal body, the central point of the perineum, which lies one inch in front of the anal orifice.

Urogenital triangle and the perineal membrane

Source: page 48

The urogenital triangle is the anterior division of the perineum. Its boundaries are:

  • Posteriorly: the imaginary transverse line between the two ischial tuberosities.
  • Anterolaterally: the pubic arches.
  • Anteriorly: the symphysis pubis.

Its contents are enclosed in two spaces, the superficial and deep perineal pouches, which are separated by the perineal membrane.

Definition

The perineal membrane is the inferior fascia of the urogenital diaphragm. It forms the inferior boundary of the deep perineal pouch and the superior boundary of the superficial pouch.

  • It lies between the urogenital diaphragm and the external genitalia.
  • It is perforated by the urethra.
  • It is attached to the posterior margin of the urogenital diaphragm and to the ischiopubic rami.

Deep perineal pouch

Source: pages 49 to 50

Boundaries

  • Roof: pelvic fascia (superficial layer of the urogenital diaphragm).
  • Floor: the perineal membrane (inferior layer of the urogenital diaphragm).
  • Laterally: the pubic arches.
  • Anteriorly and posteriorly: fusion between the roof and the floor.
High-yield

The deep perineal pouch is a completely closed pouch from all directions.

Contents

Male

  • Membranous urethra.
  • Two bulbourethral glands: small glands that secrete into the bulbar part of the urethra on erotic stimulation.
  • Muscles: external urethral sphincter (surrounds the membranous urethra) and deep transverse perinei.
  • Vessels and nerves: internal pudendal artery, dorsal nerve of penis, perineal nerve, artery of the bulb.

Female

  • Urethra.
  • Vagina, lying in the middle of the pouch.
  • Muscles: external urethral sphincter (surrounds the urethra) and deep transverse perinei.
  • Vessels and nerves: internal pudendal artery, dorsal nerve of clitoris, perineal nerve, artery of the bulb.

Superficial perineal pouch

Source: pages 51 to 53

Boundaries

  • Roof: the perineal membrane.
  • Floor: the membranous layer of superficial fascia (Colles' fascia).
  • On each side: the pubic arch.
  • Posteriorly: closed by fusion of the membranous layer of superficial fascia to the perineal membrane.
  • Anteriorly: open, continuous with the space deep to the membranous fascia of the anterior abdominal wall.

Contents

Male

  • Root of the penis: the bulb and two crura. The bulb is the posterior enlarged part of the corpus spongiosum; each crus is the posterior enlarged part of the corpus cavernosum.
  • Penile urethra inside the bulb, and the ducts of the bulbourethral glands.
  • Muscles: ischiocavernosus, bulbospongiosus and superficial transverse perinei.
  • Vessels and nerves: internal pudendal artery, dorsal nerve of penis, perineal nerve, scrotal nerves and vessels, perineal branches of S4, and perineal branches of the posterior cutaneous nerve of the thigh.

Female

  • Root of the clitoris: two crura (each the posterior enlarged part of the corpus cavernosum) and two bulbs of the vestibule.
  • Bulbs of the vestibule: erectile tissue extending on each side of the vaginal opening.
  • Vestibule of the vagina and the greater vestibular (Bartholin's) glands: two mucous glands whose small ducts open in the groove between the hymen and the posterior part of the labia minora.
  • Muscles: ischiocavernosus, bulbospongiosus and superficial transverse perinei.
  • Vessels and nerves: internal pudendal artery, dorsal nerve of clitoris, perineal nerve, labial nerves and vessels, perineal branches of S4, and perineal branches of the posterior cutaneous nerve of the thigh.

Perineal body and applied anatomy

Source: page 54

The perineal body (centrum tendinum) is the central point of the perineum. It is a fibro-muscular structure in the midline at the junction of the anus and the urogenital triangle, in both females and males, and it provides attachment to all the muscles and membranes of the perineum.

Clinical relevance

Rupture of the penile urethra (a content of the superficial pouch) by trauma lets urine extravasate into the superficial perineal pouch.

Superficial perineal pouch
Walls of scrotum and penis
Lower anterior abdominal wall
Level of umbilicus
Clinical relevance

Episiotomy is an incision through the area between the vagina and anus to enlarge the vaginal opening for obstetrical purposes during childbirth.

Check yourself

Which line divides the perineum into its two triangles?

An imaginary transverse line connecting the two ischial tuberosities.

What forms the roof and the floor of the deep perineal pouch?

The roof is pelvic fascia (superficial layer of the urogenital diaphragm); the floor is the perineal membrane.

Which parts make up the root of the penis in the superficial pouch?

The bulb and two crura.

Key Points / Quick Review

Boundaries and divisions

  • Diamond-shaped, below the pelvic diaphragm, closing the pelvic outlet.
  • Boundaries: symphysis pubis, pubic arches and sacrotuberous ligaments, coccyx.
  • Line between ischial tuberosities: anterior urogenital triangle and posterior anal triangle.

Perineal membrane

  • Inferior fascia of the urogenital diaphragm, perforated by the urethra.
  • Floor of the deep pouch and roof of the superficial pouch.

Deep pouch

  • Roof pelvic fascia, floor perineal membrane; completely closed.
  • Male: membranous urethra, bulbourethral glands. Female: urethra and vagina.
  • Both: external urethral sphincter, deep transverse perinei, internal pudendal artery, perineal nerve.

Superficial pouch

  • Roof perineal membrane, floor Colles' fascia; open anteriorly.
  • Male: root of penis, penile urethra. Female: root of clitoris, bulbs of vestibule, Bartholin's glands.
  • Both: ischiocavernosus, bulbospongiosus, superficial transverse perinei.

Perineal body and applied anatomy

  • Fibro-muscular central point; attachment for all perineal muscles and membranes.
  • Ruptured penile urethra: urine spreads to scrotum, penis and lower abdominal wall up to the umbilicus.
  • Episiotomy: incision between vagina and anus during childbirth.
