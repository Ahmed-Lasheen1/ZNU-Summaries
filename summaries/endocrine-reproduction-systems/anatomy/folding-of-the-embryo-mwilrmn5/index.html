What is folding?

Folding turns the flat embryonic disc into a cylindrical embryo by bending it ventrally.

Definition

Folding is the process of bending the embryonic disc ventrally, which transforms the flat disc into a cylindrical embryo.

  • It begins during the 3rd week and is complete during the 4th week.
  • It occurs in both the median and transverse planes, due to rapid growth of the embryo.

The four folds

Head fold

  • The cranial part of the germ disc bends ventrally to the cranial end of the notochord.
  • Carries the transverse septum, pericardium and oral membrane.

Tail fold

  • The caudal part of the germ disc bends ventrally to the caudal end of the notochord.
  • Carries the cloacal membrane and connecting stalk.

Lateral folds

  • The right and left sides of the germ disc, containing the intraembryonic coelom (I.E.C.), bend ventrally.
  • The four folds meet ventral to the embryo.

Results of folding

Shape, umbilical ring and gut

  • The embryo becomes cylindrical.
  • An opening, the primitive umbilical ring, develops in the ventral wall of the embryo.
  • Part of the secondary yolk sac is enclosed inside the body to form the gut: the foregut in the head fold, the hindgut in the tail fold and the midgut in between.
Midgut
Vitello-intestinal duct
Definitive yolk sac

The midgut communicates with the definitive yolk sac through the vitello-intestinal duct (V.I.D.), which passes through the primitive umbilical ring. As the ring narrows, the V.I.D. is constricted to form the yolk stalk, which ruptures at the end of the 5th week.

Reversal of structures

High-yield

The arrangement of structures in both the cranial and caudal parts of the germ disc is reversed by folding.

Oral membrane
Becomes the most cranial part of the embryo
Transverse septum
Lies caudal to the pericardium and heart
Cloacal membrane
Becomes the most caudal part of the embryo
Connecting stalk
Lies ventral to the embryo

Peritoneal cavity and amnion

  • Laterally, the caudal parts of the I.E.C. fuse to form a single cavity, the peritoneal cavity, around the gut.
  • Enormous expansion of the amniotic cavity obliterates the extraembryonic coelom, so the amnion forms the covering of the umbilical cord.

These pages cover the amnion and the allantois.

The amnion and amniotic fluid

In shortThe amnion is a thin sac of fluid that bathes the embryo, expands after folding to surround it, and protects and supports it.

Formation and structure

  • The amniotic cavity appears in the 2nd week as small vacuoles in the epiblast, which coalesce into a single cavity.
  • The amnion is a thin extraembryonic membrane that loosely envelops the embryo, forming an amniotic sac filled with amniotic fluid.
  • It has two layers: an outer layer from the somatopleuric layer of the extraembryonic mesoderm, and an inner layer of amniogenic cells.
  • The sac is lined by ectodermal cells of the inner cell mass (floor) and amniogenic cells (roof).

Expansion after folding

  • The amniotic cavity expands to surround the whole embryo except at the primitive umbilical ring (future umbilicus).
  • It encircles the embryo ventrally, merging around the umbilical cord, and forms the epithelial layer of the cord.
  • With growth of the embryo, the amnion obliterates the chorionic cavity.

Amniotic fluid

Composition: 98% water and 2% inorganic and organic salts, proteins and enzymes.

Functions

  1. Protection against external trauma, thermal trauma and adhesions.
  2. Allows learning of swallowing and suckling.
  3. Medium for excretion by the kidneys and intestine.
  4. Provides nutrients: glucose, minerals and amino acids.
  5. Allows free movement, which aids neuromuscular development.
  6. Allows symmetrical growth of the baby.
  7. Stimulates peristalsis.
  8. During labour: dilates the internal os, acts as a lubricant and sterilizes the vagina.

Amniotic fluid abnormalities

Definition

Amniocentesis is a procedure performed during pregnancy to obtain amniotic fluid, to test for chromosomal abnormalities and fetal infections.

Oligohydramnios

  • Too little fluid: less than half a litre (400 mL in the third trimester).
  • Leads to adhesion.
  • Causes: renal agenesis, obstruction of the urinary tract.

Polyhydramnios

  • Too much fluid: more than 2000 mL.
  • Leads to premature labour.
  • Causes: esophageal atresia, anencephaly.

Cul du sac: the fetus is born in a closed amniotic sac.

The allantois

  • The allantois appears on the 16th day as a sausage-shaped, dorso-caudal endodermal diverticulum of the secondary yolk sac, extending into the connecting stalk (future umbilical cord).
  • After folding it shifts to the ventral aspect of the embryo as a content of the umbilical cord and is continuous with the hindgut (cloaca).

Fate

Proximal part
Forms the apex of the urinary bladder
Distal part (urachus)
Forms the median umbilical ligament after birth, from the bladder apex to the umbilicus
Surrounding mesoderm
Gives rise to the umbilical vessels

The allantois also assists in early blood formation.

Key Points / Quick Review

Folding

  • Ventral bending of the disc turns it into a cylindrical embryo, from the 3rd to the 4th week.
  • Head fold: transverse septum, pericardium, oral membrane. Tail fold: cloacal membrane, connecting stalk.
  • Four folds meet ventrally, leaving the primitive umbilical ring.

Gut and reversal

  • Foregut in the head fold, hindgut in the tail fold, midgut in between.
  • V.I.D. becomes the yolk stalk, which ruptures at the end of the 5th week.
  • Oral membrane most cranial, cloacal membrane most caudal, transverse septum caudal to the heart.

Amnion

  • Outer somatopleuric layer, inner amniogenic cells; forms the epithelial covering of the cord.
  • Fluid: 98% water; 30 mL at 10 weeks, 350 mL at 20 weeks, 700 to 1000 mL at 37 weeks.
  • Oligohydramnios: renal agenesis, urinary obstruction. Polyhydramnios: esophageal atresia, anencephaly.

Allantois

  • Endodermal diverticulum of the secondary yolk sac, appears on day 16.
  • Proximal part forms the apex of the bladder; distal part (urachus) forms the median umbilical ligament.
  • Its mesoderm gives the umbilical vessels.
