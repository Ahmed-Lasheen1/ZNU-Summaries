Structure of the breast

The breast is formed from the mammary gland, its surrounding fat, the overlying skin and the underlying deep fascia. Mammary glands are modified sweat glands capable of secreting milk, and they are present in both sexes.

Definition

The mammary glands are modified sweat glands that are capable of secreting milk.

The breast is conical in shape and has three parts:

  • Apex, the nipple, which is a conical projection about the middle of the breast.
  • Base, which is wide and lies on the chest wall.
  • Axillary tail, a small part that extends upwards and laterally along the lower border of the pectoralis major, pierces the deep fascia and enters the axilla.

The base and its relations

The base is wide. It extends from the 2nd to the 6th rib, and from the lateral margin of the sternum to the mid-axillary line.

The base rests on muscles, as follows:

Pectoralis major
Behind the upper two-thirds of the breast
Serratus anterior and external abdominal oblique
Behind the lower third of the breast

The base is separated from these muscles by the deep fascia and a layer of loose areolar tissue. This layer is the retromammary space, and it normally lets the breast move freely over the muscles.

Nipple, areola and glandular tissue

The nipple lies at the level of the 4th intercostal space in nulliparous women. It contains no fat and is surrounded by a pigmented area of skin called the areola. The areola is pinkish in nulliparous women and becomes dark brown after the first pregnancy.

The glandular part consists of 15 to 20 lobes radiating from the nipple, embedded in the superficial fascia. Each lobe gives a lactiferous duct. Each duct dilates to form a lactiferous sinus before it opens separately on the nipple.

Because the lactiferous ducts radiate out from the nipple beneath the areola, incisions in the breast are made in a radial direction.

The non-glandular part is fat. It covers the glands, forms the main bulk of the breast, and gives the breast its normal contour and soft consistency.

Fibrous septa called the suspensory ligaments of Cooper extend from the skin to the deep fascia between the lobes. Each compartment they form contains one lobe only.

Blood supply and lymphatic drainage

Arterial supply

  • Branches of the internal mammary artery, which arises from the first part of the subclavian artery.
  • The lateral thoracic artery, which arises from the second part of the axillary artery.
  • Branches of the intercostal arteries.

The veins of the breast accompany its arteries.

Lymphatic drainage

The breast is divided into four quadrants, and each drains as follows:

Medial two quadrants
Parasternal nodes, mainly of the same side and partly of the opposite side
Lateral two quadrants
Pectoral (anterior) group of axillary nodes
Upper part
Apical group of axillary nodes
Lower part
Lymph vessels of the rectus sheath
Clinical relevance

In malignancy, Cooper's ligaments become shortened and pull on the overlying skin, so the skin becomes dimpled and the nipple may be retracted. Normally the breast is mobile over the muscles, but in advanced malignancy the muscles are infiltrated and the breast becomes fixed.

Development and congenital anomalies

The mammary glands develop from a mammary line of ectodermal ridges that extends from the axilla, at the level of the forelimb, to the inguinal region, at the level of the hind limb.

  1. Ridge formation

    The ectodermal mammary ridge appears between the axilla and the inguinal region.

  2. Regression

    The ridge disappears everywhere except in the thoracic region.

  3. Solid cords

    The remaining ridge penetrates the underlying mesenchyme and forms 15 to 20 solid cords.

  4. Milk ducts

    These cords are canalized to form the future milk ducts.

Key Points / Quick Review

Structure

  • Mammary glands are modified sweat glands that secrete milk, present in both sexes.
  • The breast has an apex (nipple), a base and an axillary tail.
  • The base extends from the 2nd to the 6th rib.

Nipple and glandular tissue

  • The nipple lies at the 4th intercostal space in nulliparous women and contains no fat.
  • The glandular part has 15 to 20 lobes with lactiferous sinuses.
  • Incisions are made radially.

Supply and drainage

  • Arteries: internal mammary, lateral thoracic and intercostal arteries.
  • Lymph: medial to parasternal, lateral to pectoral, upper to apical, lower to rectus sheath.

Development

  • The mammary line runs from the axilla to the inguinal region.
  • The ridge persists in the thoracic region and forms 15 to 20 cords that canalize into milk ducts.
