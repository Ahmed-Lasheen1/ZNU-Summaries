Structure of the islets

The islets of Langerhans are multi-hormonal endocrine micro-organs scattered through the pancreas.

  • There are more than one million islets in the pancreas.
  • They appear as pale cellular clusters embedded within the exocrine pancreatic acini, and are more abundant in the tail.
  • Most islets are 100 to 200 µm in diameter and contain several hundred cells.
  • Each islet is surrounded by a delicate loose connective tissue capsule.
  • In section, the cells are polygonal or rounded and arranged in cords separated by a network of fenestrated blood capillaries.
  • Both the parenchymal cells and the blood vessels are innervated by autonomic nerve fibers.

Cell types and their products

Immunocytochemical methods have located seven cell types in the islets: α, β, D (delta), F, G, C and ganglion cells.

Alpha and beta cells compared

The two main islet cell types differ in position, size, staining and granule appearance. Both contain RER and a well-developed Golgi apparatus.

Alpha (α) cells

  • Located mainly at the periphery
  • Larger, 15 to 25 µm in diameter
  • Acidophilic granular cytoplasm
  • Regular granules with a dense core and a narrow electron-lucent region

Beta (β) cells

  • Scattered, but mostly in the center
  • Oval, 10 to 15 µm in diameter
  • Basophilic cytoplasm
  • Irregular granules with a dense core and a wide electron-lucent region

How glucagon raises blood glucose

When blood glucose is low, alpha cells release glucagon, which acts on liver cells.

Beta cells do the opposite: they synthesize proinsulin in the RER and secrete insulin, which decreases blood glucose. Delta cells, with electron-lucent homogeneous granules, secrete somatostatin, which lowers the secretion of both insulin and glucagon.

Check yourself

Which islet cells lie mainly at the periphery, and which are the most numerous?

Alpha cells are mainly peripheral (about 20%); beta cells are the most numerous (70%) and lie mostly in the center.

What is the effect of somatostatin from delta cells on islet secretion?

It decreases the rate of secretion of both insulin and glucagon, and it also reduces intestinal motility.

What are the roles of G cells and PP cells?

G cells secrete gastrin, which stimulates HCl production by parietal cells; PP cells secrete pancreatic polypeptide, which inhibits exocrine pancreatic secretion.

Key Points / Quick Review

Islets

  • More than one million islets, more abundant in the tail, embedded in exocrine acini.
  • Most are 100 to 200 µm with several hundred cells and a delicate loose connective tissue capsule.
  • Cells in cords separated by fenestrated capillaries; autonomic innervation of cells and vessels.
  • Seven cell types: α, β, D, F, G, C and ganglion cells.

Alpha and beta cells

  • α: peripheral, about 20%, 15 to 25 µm, acidophilic, releases glucagon when blood glucose is low.
  • β: 70%, mostly central, 10 to 15 µm, basophilic, makes proinsulin and secretes insulin.
  • α granules: dense core, narrow lucent region; β granules: irregular, dense core, wide lucent region.

Other cells

  • Delta (about 5%): somatostatin lowers insulin and glucagon secretion and reduces intestinal motility.
  • G: gastrin stimulates gastric HCl production.
  • PP (F): pancreatic polypeptide inhibits exocrine pancreatic secretion.
  • C (3%): scanty granules, possibly a reserve or resting cell.
  • Ganglion cells: control the secretory activity of the gland.
