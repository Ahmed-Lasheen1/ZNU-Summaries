What a reflex is

Definition

A reflex is a fast, automatic and stereotyped response to a stimulus. It does not need conscious thought.

The nervous pathway that carries out a reflex is called the reflex arc. Every reflex arc has the same basic parts, shown in the next section.

High-yield

Know the five parts of the reflex arc in order. It is a very common exam question.

The five steps of the arc

  1. Receptor

    Detects the stimulus and starts a signal.

  2. Afferent neuron

    Carries the signal toward the central nervous system.

  3. Integration center

    Processes the signal inside the central nervous system.

  4. Efferent neuron

    Carries the response away from the central nervous system.

  5. Effector

    A muscle or gland that produces the response.

Don't confuse

Afferent neurons arrive at the central nervous system. Efferent neurons exit from it.

Two kinds of reflex

Monosynaptic

  • One synapse in the central nervous system
  • The afferent neuron connects directly to the efferent neuron
  • Example: the knee-jerk reflex

Polysynaptic

  • One or more interneurons in between
  • More synapses, so a slightly longer delay
  • Example: the withdrawal reflex
Clinical relevance

Doctors test reflexes such as the knee jerk to check that the parts of the reflex arc are working.

Check yourself

Which part of the reflex arc detects the stimulus?

The receptor.

What is the difference between afferent and efferent neurons?

Afferent neurons carry signals toward the central nervous system. Efferent neurons carry them away from it.

Why does a polysynaptic reflex have a slightly longer delay?

It has more synapses, because one or more interneurons sit between the afferent and efferent neurons.

Key Points / Quick Review

Reflex basics

  • A reflex is fast, automatic and stereotyped
  • The pathway is called the reflex arc

The five parts

  • Receptor, afferent neuron, integration center, efferent neuron, effector

Two types

  • Monosynaptic: one synapse, for example the knee jerk
  • Polysynaptic: interneurons involved, for example withdrawal
