Station 1 (6 marks)

  1. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Pain sensation.

  2. What is the tool used? (1 mark)

    Sterile examination pin.

  3. What are the receptors of pain? (1 mark)

    Free nerve endings.

  4. What is the tract that carries pain? (1 mark)

    Lateral spinothalamic tract.

  5. Name the tract of fast pain and of slow pain. (1 mark)

    Fast: neospinothalamic. Slow: paleospinothalamic.

  6. Mention the general rules of sensory examination. (1 mark)

    Patient’s eyes closed; area exposed; bilateral (compare both sides); limbs from distal to proximal; follow dermatomal distribution.

Station 2 (7 marks)

  1. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Temperature sensation.

  2. What are the tools used? (1 mark)

    Two test tubes: cold (10–15°C) and hot (35–40°C). A cold metal object (tuning fork) can test cold.

  3. Warm sensation: range, receptors and fibers? (1 mark)

    30–45°C; free nerve endings; slow C fibers.

  4. Cold sensation: range, receptors and fibers? (1 mark)

    10–30°C; Krause’s end bulbs; fast A fibers.

  5. What is the tract of temperature? (1 mark)

    Lateral spinothalamic tract.

  6. What is paradoxical cold sensation? Explain. (1 mark)

    On a hot shower at 45°C the person first feels cold then warm, because cold receptors are 10× more numerous, more superficial, and re-fire at 45°C.

  7. Types of thermoreceptors? (1 mark)

    Internal (hypothalamus) and external (cold and warm receptors).

Station 3 (6 marks)

  1. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Crude touch.

  2. What is the tool? (1 mark)

    Brush or a piece of cotton.

  3. How should the stimulus be applied? (1 mark)

    Gently tap or dab the skin; avoid stroking or tickling.

  4. What are the receptors? (1 mark)

    Free nerve endings and hair end organ.

  5. Which nerve fiber transmits it? (1 mark)

    A delta fiber.

  6. What is the tract? (1 mark)

    Ventral spinothalamic tract.

Station 4 (4 marks)

  1. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Tactile localization (topognosis).

  2. What is the tool? (1 mark)

    A blunt tool with a point, like a pen.

  3. What is asked from the patient? (1 mark)

    If he felt the sensation, and to point to the site of the previously touched point.

  4. What is the type of this sensation and its tract? (1 mark)

    Fine touch; gracile and cuneate tracts.

Station 5 (8 marks)

  1. Name the instrument. (1 mark)

    Weber’s compass.

  2. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Tactile discrimination (two-point discrimination).

  3. Define tactile discrimination. (1 mark)

    Ability of a closed-eye person to differentiate between touch at one point and simultaneous touch at two separate points.

  4. Threshold at finger tips / lips and at the back? (1 mark)

    2 mm / 15 mm.

  5. Mention the 3 requirements of a part with sensitive tactile discrimination. (1 mark)

    Very crowded receptors; many sensory nerves (no convergence); wide cortical representation.

  6. What are the receptors? (1 mark)

    Merkel’s, Meissner’s and basket hair endings.

  7. What is the tract? (1 mark)

    Gracile and cuneate tracts.

  8. Where is point-to-point discrimination most accurate? (1 mark)

    Fovea centralis of the retina (light discrimination, not tactile).

Station 6 (4 marks)

  1. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Sense of position and movement.

  2. What is the type of this sensation? (1 mark)

    Deep (kinesthetic / proprioceptive).

  3. Name the tract. (1 mark)

    Gracile and cuneate tracts.

  4. Mention the other deep sensations. (1 mark)

    Muscle pressure and muscle tension.

Station 7 (5 marks)

  1. The left picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Sense of muscle pressure.

  2. The right picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Pressure: arm supported. Tension: arm suspended.

  5. Name the tract. (1 mark)

Station 8 (4 marks)

  1. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Vibration sense.

  2. What is the tool? (1 mark)

    Tuning fork.

  3. What is the type of this sensation and its tract? (1 mark)

    Mixed (cutaneous + deep); gracile and cuneate tracts.

  4. Mention two uses of the tuning fork. (1 mark)

Station 9 (7 marks)

  1. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Stereognosis.

  2. Define stereognosis. (1 mark)

    Ability to recognize previously known objects in the palm without vision.

  3. What is its character? (1 mark)

    A type of fine touch needing both cutaneous and deep receptors (mixed).

  4. What is its center? (1 mark)

    Areas 5 and 7 (sensory association area).

  5. What is its tract? (1 mark)

    Gracile and cuneate tracts.

  6. Define astereognosis. (1 mark)

    Loss of the sense of stereognosis.

  7. Mention three causes of astereognosis. (1 mark)

    Lesion of areas 5,7; tabes dorsalis (syphilis, destroys dorsal roots); cutting gracile and cuneate tracts; pernicious anemia (low B12, demyelination of ascending tracts).

Station 10 (6 marks)

  1. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Plantar reflex.

  2. What is the type of this reflex? (1 mark)

    Superficial spinal reflex.

  3. What is the stimulus? (1 mark)

    Scratching the lateral aspect of the sole with a blunt object.

  4. What is the normal response? (1 mark)

    Plantar flexion of the big toe and other toes.

  5. What does the normal response mean? (1 mark)

    Intact pyramidal and extrapyramidal systems.

  6. What is the center? (1 mark)

    S1.

Station 11 (5 marks)

  1. What is the name of this sign? (1 mark)

    Babinski’s sign.

  2. What is the abnormal response? (1 mark)

    Dorsiflexion of the big toe with fanning of other toes.

  3. Dorsiflexion of the big toe indicates …? (1 mark)

    Pyramidal lesion.

  4. Fanning of the other toes indicates …? (1 mark)

    Extrapyramidal lesion.

  5. Mention conditions where Babinski’s sign is normal. (1 mark)

    Newborn (lack of myelination), deep sleep, coma, anesthesia.

Station 12 (2 marks)

  1. Name this instrument. (1 mark)

    Medical (reflex) hammer.

  2. What is the type of tendon jerk? (1 mark)

    Deep, spinal, monosynaptic reflex.

Station 13 (4 marks)

  1. What is the name of this reflex? (1 mark)

    Biceps reflex.

  2. What is its center? (1 mark)

    C5, C6.

  3. Describe the procedure. (1 mark)

    Tap the biceps indirectly while forearm is semi-flexed, elbow at 120°.

  4. What is the response? (1 mark)

    Mild contraction of biceps with slight flexion of the elbow.

Station 14 (4 marks)

  1. What is the name of this reflex? (1 mark)

    Triceps reflex.

  2. What is its center? (1 mark)

    C6, C7.

  3. Describe the procedure. (1 mark)

    Tap the triceps directly while the elbow is flexed at 90°.

  4. What is the response? (1 mark)

    Mild contraction of triceps and extension of the elbow.

Station 15 (4 marks)

  1. What is the name of this reflex? (1 mark)

    Knee jerk.

  2. What is its center? (1 mark)

    L2, L3, L4.

  3. Describe the procedure. (1 mark)

    Tap the patellar tendon midway between origin and insertion while hip and knee are flexed.

  4. What is the response? (1 mark)

    Contraction of quadriceps and extension of the knee.

Station 16 (4 marks)

  1. What is the name of this reflex? (1 mark)

    Ankle jerk.

  2. What is its center? (1 mark)

    S1, S2.

  3. Describe the procedure. (1 mark)

    Tap tendo Achilles while hip is abducted and externally rotated, knee flexed at 90°, ankle dorsiflexed.

  4. What is the response? (1 mark)

    Mild contraction of calf muscles with plantar flexion of the ankle.

Station 17 (4 marks)

  1. Mention two causes of absent tendon jerk (areflexia). (1 mark)

    Advanced tabes dorsalis (afferent lesion); lower motor neuron lesion (efferent lesion).

  2. Mention two causes of hyporeflexia. (1 mark)

    Hypothyroidism (myxedema); coma, anesthesia, shock, sleep.

  3. Mention four causes of hyperreflexia. (1 mark)

    Upper motor neuron lesion; paleocerebellar syndrome; hyperthyroidism; tetany; anxiety; eclampsia.

  4. What is pendular knee jerk? Mention two causes. (1 mark)

    Knee jerk with repeated swings, in hypotonia: neocerebellar syndrome, chorea, thalamic syndrome, pure motor area 4 lesion, pure pyramidal lesion.

Station 18 (2 marks)

  1. Mention the routes of sound transmission. (1 mark)

    Ossicular, bone and air conduction.

  2. Which is better: ossicular or bone conduction? (1 mark)

    Ossicular conduction.

Station 19 (5 marks)

  1. What type of deafness is shown in the left / right picture? (1 mark)

    Left: conductive deafness. Right: nerve (perceptive, sensorineural) deafness.

  2. Define conductive deafness. (1 mark)

    Interference with conduction of sound waves from the external ear to the oval window.

  3. Mention its criteria. (1 mark)

    Bone conduction > ossicular conduction; patient speaks in a low voice (hears his sound magnified).

  4. Define nerve deafness. (1 mark)

    Interference with auditory transmission from the oval window to the auditory cortex.

  5. Mention its criteria. (1 mark)

    Ossicular > bone conduction but both reduced; patient speaks in a high voice.

Station 20 (5 marks)

  1. Which hearing test is shown? (1 mark)

    Rinne’s test.

  2. What does it compare? (1 mark)

    Ossicular vs bone conduction in the same person, each ear separately.

  3. What is the normal result? (1 mark)

    Ossicular better than bone for ~45 sec (Rinne +ve).

  4. Result in conductive deafness? (1 mark)

    Bone better than ossicular (Rinne –ve).

  5. Result in perceptive deafness? (1 mark)

    Ossicular better than bone but both reduced (reduced Rinne).

Station 21 (5 marks)

  1. Which hearing test is shown? (1 mark)

    Weber test.

  2. What does it compare? (1 mark)

    Bone conduction between the two ears in the same person.

  3. What is the normal result? (1 mark)

    Sound heard equally in both ears.

  4. Result in conductive deafness? Why? (1 mark)

    Louder in the diseased ear (absent masking effect of noise).

  5. Result in perceptive deafness? (1 mark)

    Louder in the normal ear.

Station 22 (5 marks)

  1. Which hearing test is shown? (1 mark)

    Schwabach’s test.

  2. What does it compare? (1 mark)

    Bone conduction between patient and examiner (examiner must be normal).

  3. What is the normal result? (1 mark)

    Equal time of hearing.

  4. Result in conductive deafness? (1 mark)

    Patient is better.

  5. Result in perceptive deafness? (1 mark)

    Examiner is better.

Station 23 (8 marks)

  1. This picture shows an examination of ………… (1 mark)

    Pupillary light reflex.

  2. What is the tool? (1 mark)

    A pen torch.

  3. What is the stimulus? (1 mark)

    Light exposed to one eye.

  4. What is the response? (1 mark)

    Same pupil constricts (direct) and the other pupil constricts (indirect / consensual).

  5. Light reflex is lost in …? (1 mark)

    Encephalitis and alcoholism.

  6. What is Argyll Robertson pupil? (1 mark)

    Pupil that fails to respond to light but responds to accommodation.

  7. What is reverse Argyll Robertson pupil? (1 mark)

    Pupil reacts to light but not to near accommodation.

  8. What is seen in Horner’s syndrome? (1 mark)

    Persistently constricted pupil.

Station 24 (8 marks)

  1. What is the name of this reflex? (1 mark)

    Accommodation reflex.

  2. What is its stimulus? (1 mark)

    Blurred image on the retina.

  3. Mention the three components of the response. (1 mark)

    Miosis of both pupils; medial convergence of both eyes; increased convexity of the lens.

  4. Mention values of miosis. (1 mark)

    Increases depth of focus; focuses image on fovea; light rays fall on corresponding retinal points; protects from excess light; prevents chromatic and spherical aberration.

  5. Value of medial convergence? (1 mark)

    Brings the images on the fovea centralis of both eyes.

  6. Value of increased lens convexity? (1 mark)

    Increases its dioptric power.

  7. Which muscles are responsible? (1 mark)

    Ciliary muscle (accommodation), sphincter pupillae (constriction), bilateral medial recti (convergence).

  8. Which lens is for near vision? (1 mark)

    The rounded lens (flattened lens for far vision).

Station 25 (8 marks)

  1. Name the two charts. (1 mark)

    Snellen’s letter chart and Landolt’s broken circle chart.

  2. What do they measure? Define it. (1 mark)

    Visual acuity: ability of the eye to differentiate two separate points and detect fine details.

  3. What is required to see two points separate? (1 mark)

    They stimulate two separate cones with one unstimulated cone between.

  4. What is the visual angle? (1 mark)

    1 minute (1/60°).

  5. Where is acuity maximal? (1 mark)

    Fovea.

  6. At what distance, and how is each eye tested? (1 mark)

    6 meters (no accommodation); each eye separately; from above downward.

  7. How is the result expressed? (1 mark)

    N/D: numerator = distance of patient from chart; denominator = distance at which a normal person can see it.

  8. What does 6/18 mean? (1 mark)

    The patient sees at 6 m what a normal eye sees at 18 m.

Station 26 (4 marks)

  1. Classify sensations. (1 mark)

    Somatic (cutaneous: pain, temperature, touch; deep: position & movement, muscle pressure, tension; mixed: stereognosis, vibration), visceral, organic (hypothalamic).

  2. Complete the tract of: pain, temperature, crude touch, fine touch, deep, mixed. (1 mark)

    Lateral spinothalamic; lateral spinothalamic; ventral spinothalamic; gracile & cuneate; gracile & cuneate; gracile & cuneate.

  3. Compare conductive vs perceptive deafness in Rinne, Weber, Schwabach. (1 mark)

    Conductive: Rinne –ve, louder in diseased ear, patient better. Perceptive: reduced Rinne, louder in normal ear, examiner better.

  4. Classify reflexes and give an example of each. (1 mark)

    Superficial: plantar reflex. Deep: tendon jerks (biceps, triceps, knee, ankle).
