The testis

The male genital system is composed of the testis (gonads), excretory ducts, accessory sex glands and external genitalia.

The testes are the primary male sex organs: a pair of ovoid organs lying within the scrotum.

High-yield

The testis has two functions: production of sperms (exocrine) and synthesis of testosterone (endocrine).

Blood supply and lymphatics

  • Arterial: testicular arteries from the abdominal aorta at the L2 vertebra.
  • Venous: the pampiniform plexus on both sides, then the right testicular vein drains into the IVC, and the left testicular vein into the left renal vein, then the IVC.
  • Lymphatic: into the para-aortic lymph nodes.

Male genital ducts: extratesticular

These are the epididymis, ductus deferens and ejaculatory duct.

Ductus epididymis

The epididymis is a crescent-shaped, highly convoluted tube about 6 m long that connects the efferent ductules to the vas deferens. It lies on the superior and posterolateral surface of the testis and has a head, body and tail. Its blood supply and lymphatic drainage are as for the testis.

Ductus (vas) deferens

The vas deferens is the longest part of the extratesticular ducts: a fibromuscular tube connecting the tail of the epididymis with the prostatic urethra, transporting spermatozoa to the ejaculatory ducts.

  • It begins at the inferior pole of the testis as a direct continuation of the duct of the epididymis.
  • It runs upward in the spermatic cord, through the inguinal canal, into the abdominal cavity.
  • Its terminal part is sacculated to form the ampulla of the vas deferens.

Ejaculatory duct

The terminal narrow part of the vas plus the duct of the seminal vesicle form the ejaculatory duct at the base of the prostate. It opens in the prostatic urethra.

The spermatic cord

The spermatic cord is a collection of vessels, nerves and ducts running to and from the testes, surrounded by fascia to form a cord-like structure.

Posterior border of testis

At the posterior border of the testis its contents disperse to supply the testes and scrotum.

Contents

  1. Vas deferens.
  2. Artery of the vas deferens.
  3. Testicular arteries (from the abdominal aorta).
  4. Pampiniform plexus of veins, forming the testicular veins.
  5. Autonomic nerve fibers of the testis.
  6. Lymph vessels.
  7. Cremasteric artery.
  8. Cremasteric nerve (genital branch of the genitofemoral nerve).
  9. Remnants of the processus vaginalis.

Prostate: lobes and supply

  • Five lobes: anterior (in front of the urethra), posterior, median (between the urethra and the two ejaculatory ducts), and two lateral (right and left, on each side of the urethra).
  • Arterial supply: inferior vesical and inferior rectal arteries.
Clinical relevance

About the age of fifty years the median lobe may enlarge and press on the sphincter vesicae at the bladder neck, obstructing the flow of urine.

Scrotum

The scrotum is an out-pouching of the lower part of the anterior abdominal wall, divided by a median raphe into 2 compartments. Each contains the testis, the epididymis and the lower part of the spermatic cord.

  1. SkinWrinkled and thin
  2. Superficial fasciaNo fat; contains dartos muscle
  3. External spermatic fascia
  4. Cremasteric fascia
  5. Internal spermatic fascia

The dartos muscle (involuntary muscle) is responsible for wrinkling of the scrotal skin.

Blood supply

  • Arterial: external pudendal branches from the femoral artery, and the internal pudendal artery from the internal iliac artery.
  • Venous: corresponding to the arteries.

Check yourself

Which structures form the ejaculatory duct, and where does it open?

The terminal part of the vas deferens and the duct of the seminal vesicle form it; it opens in the prostatic urethra.

What is the course of the spermatic cord?

It forms at the deep inguinal ring, passes through the inguinal canal and superficial inguinal ring into the scrotum, and reaches the posterior border of the testis.

Which prostatic lobe enlarges at about fifty years, and what does it cause?

The median lobe; it presses on the sphincter vesicae at the bladder neck and obstructs urine flow.

Key Points / Quick Review

Testis

  • Exocrine (sperms) and endocrine (testosterone) functions.
  • Artery from the aorta at L2; right vein to IVC, left vein to left renal vein; lymph to para-aortic nodes.

Ducts

  • Intratesticular: tubuli recti, rete testis, efferent ductules (5 to 6, form the head of the epididymis).
  • Epididymis (about 6 m), then vas deferens, ampulla, ejaculatory duct into the prostatic urethra.

Spermatic cord

  • Deep inguinal ring to the posterior border of the testis.
  • Nine contents, including the vas, testicular artery, pampiniform plexus and the cremasteric nerve.

Accessory glands

  • Seminal vesicle: 10 to 15 cm, posteroinferior to the bladder.
  • Prostate: inverted cone, five lobes; median lobe enlargement obstructs urine flow.
  • Bulbourethral glands: empty into the proximal penile urethra.

Scrotum

  • Skin, superficial fascia (dartos, no fat), then three spermatic fasciae.
  • Supplied by external pudendal and internal pudendal arteries.
