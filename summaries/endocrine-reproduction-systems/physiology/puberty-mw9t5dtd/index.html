Hormonal changes at puberty

Pulsatile GnRH secretion comes first and precedes the physical (phenotypic) changes.

  • In young children, LH and FSH levels are insufficient to initiate gonadal function.
  • At puberty, LH and FSH blood levels and pulse amplitude increase, especially during sleep (nocturnal pulsatility). These high levels initiate gonadal development.
  • Growth hormone (GH) secretion from the anterior pituitary increases.
  • TSH secretion increases in both sexes: it increases the metabolic rate and promotes tissue growth.

Girls

  • Increased LH and FSH initiate the female cycle; the LH surge usually is not sufficient to cause ovulation in the 1st cycle.
  • Rising estrogen causes growth of the pelvis, internal reproductive organs and external genitalia.
  • Estrogen also causes subcutaneous fat deposition in the breast, thigh and gluteal region.
  • Adrenal androgens increase: pubic hair growth, bone growth and increased sebaceous gland secretion (predisposes to acne).

Boys

  • Increased LH and FSH stimulate spermatogenesis.
  • Adrenal androgens initiate growth of sex accessory structures (e.g. prostate) and male secondary sex characteristics (e.g. facial hair, growth of the larynx).
  • Androgens cause mineral retention (supporting bone and muscle growth), Ca2+ deposition in bones and increased muscle bulk (anabolic effect: increased protein synthesis).
  • Some estrogens are secreted by Sertoli cells.

Theories of the onset of puberty

  1. Maturation of the CNS and hypothalamus: gradual establishment of pulsatile GnRH release from hypothalamic neurons.
  2. Change in sensitivity to gonadal (and/or adrenal) steroids: the set point of their negative feedback on FSH and LH gradually increases (less sensitive to feedback), and the anterior pituitary becomes more sensitive to hypothalamic GnRH.
  3. Pineal melatonin: its inhibitory effect on the anterior pituitary and gonads is removed after the pineal gland calcifies.
  4. Blood glucose and free fatty acids: all female mammals must reach a certain body size before puberty starts, and a certain degree of fatness before reproductive cycles start.
  5. Leptin: produced by adipocytes, it stimulates GnRH neurons directly or through neuropeptide Y neurons. Blood leptin reflects nutritional status: more fat, more leptin.
  6. External factors: environmental and social factors.

Abnormal puberty

Delayed puberty

Puberty occurring at an unusually late age. It is divided by stature.

Without short stature

  • Constitutional
  • Gonadotropin deficiency (e.g. hypothalamic and pituitary tumors)
  • Gonadal disorders (infections, trauma, post-surgical resection)
  • Androgen receptor defects
  • Chronic disease (congenital heart disease, asthma, anorexia, hyperthyroidism)

With short stature

  • Constitutional
  • Panhypopituitarism
  • Glucocorticoid excess (Cushing syndrome)
  • Chronic disease (congenital heart disease, asthma, chronic renal failure, hypothyroidism, diabetes)

Precocious puberty

Definition

Precocious puberty is the development of secondary sexual characteristics before 8 years in girls or 9 years in boys.

Precocious true puberty

  • Gonadotropin dependent
  • Cause: organic brain disease (e.g. hypothalamic tumors) affecting GnRH secretion
  • Early spermatogenesis in boys and ovarian development in girls

Precocious pseudo-puberty

  • Gonadotropin independent
  • No spermatogenesis or ovarian development, as there is no increase in GnRH
  • FSH and LH blood levels are lower than normal
High-yield

Causes of precocious pseudo-puberty: congenital adrenal hyperplasia (large amounts of androgens: increased pubic and axillary hair, enlargement of male sexual organs) and sex hormone-secreting tumors. An adrenal tumor presents with hirsutism, acne and enlarged sexual organs; an ovarian tumor makes females show secondary sexual characters.

Adrenogenital syndrome (congenital adrenal hyperplasia)

Excessive production of sex hormones, mainly androgens, from the adrenal cortex due to an inherited disorder.

Check yourself

What happens to the set point of negative feedback by gonadal steroids at puberty?

It gradually increases, so the hypothalamus and pituitary become less sensitive to the feedback, while the pituitary becomes more sensitive to GnRH.

Which type of precocious puberty shows no gametogenesis, and what are the FSH and LH levels?

Precocious pseudo-puberty: there is no increase in GnRH, and FSH and LH are lower than normal.

Why is a boy with adrenogenital syndrome short in childhood?

Because of early union of the epiphysis.

Key Points / Quick Review

Hormonal changes

  • Pulsatile GnRH precedes physical changes; LH and FSH pulses rise, especially during sleep.
  • GH and TSH also increase.
  • Girls: estrogen (pelvis, genitalia, fat); adrenal androgens (pubic hair, bone growth, acne).
  • Boys: LH and FSH start spermatogenesis; adrenal androgens and anabolic androgens; Sertoli cells secrete some estrogens.

Theories of onset

  • CNS and hypothalamic maturation; raised feedback set point with a more sensitive pituitary.
  • Pineal melatonin inhibition removed after calcification.
  • Body size and fatness, glucose and FFA; leptin from adipocytes; external factors.

Abnormal puberty

  • Delayed: with or without short stature.
  • Precocious: before 8 years (girls) or 9 years (boys).
  • True: gonadotropin dependent. Pseudo: gonadotropin independent, low FSH and LH.

Adrenogenital syndrome

  • Excess adrenal androgens due to an inherited disorder.
  • Males: macro-genitosomia precox, then precocious pseudo-puberty.
  • Females: pseudo hermaphroditism, musculinization, then virilism.
