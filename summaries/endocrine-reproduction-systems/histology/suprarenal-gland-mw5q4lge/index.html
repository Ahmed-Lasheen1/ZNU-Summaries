Suprarenal gland
At a glance

The suprarenal (adrenal) gland has a supporting stroma, a steroid-secreting cortex arranged in concentric zones, a catecholamine-secreting medulla, and a special microcirculation linking the two.

Stroma and parenchyma

The gland is formed of a supporting stroma and a secretory parenchyma arranged as a cortex and a medulla.

  • Capsule: thick, covered with renal adipose connective tissue.
  • Trabeculae: thin septa descending from the capsule. They divide the cortex into compartments and carry blood vessels and nerves.
  • Reticular fibers: a well-developed network of fibers and cells supporting the parenchymal cells of both cortex and medulla.
  • Parenchyma: secretory cells arranged in the cortex and the medulla.

Adrenal cortex

The cortex contains cells that synthesize and secrete several steroid hormones.

Adrenal secretions are not stored in the secretory cells; they are synthesized and released as needed. The cortex has three concentric zones, named according to the arrangement of their cells: zona glomerulosa, zona fasciculata and zona reticularis.

Zona glomerulosa

  • Narrow zone directly under the capsule, made of columnar cells with deeply stained oval nuclei.
  • Cytoplasm is acidophilic with clumps of basophilic material.
  • EM (steroid-secreting cell): electron-dense nuclei with one or two prominent nucleoli, well-developed SER, many elongated mitochondria with tubular cristae, few lipid droplets, few RER and free ribosomes.
  • The Golgi complex tends to polarize toward the nearest blood capillaries.

Zona fasciculata

  • Polyhedral cells with rounded nuclei and faint acidophilic cytoplasm.
  • The cytoplasm looks highly vacuolated (spongy) because lipid droplets dissolve during histological preparation, so the cells are called spongiocytes.
  • EM: abundant SER, many lipid droplets, numerous mitochondria with tubular cristae, few RER and free ribosomes.

Zona reticularis

  • Polyhedral cells with dark nuclei and acidophilic cytoplasm.
  • EM: abundant SER, elongated mitochondria with tubular cristae, few lipid droplets and lipofuscin pigments, small Golgi apparatus, few RER and free ribosomes.
High-yield

All three zones show the steroid-secreting pattern: abundant SER, mitochondria with tubular cristae, and few RER.

Adrenal medulla

The medulla is ectodermal in origin (neural crest). It is composed of endocrine chromaffin cells and nerve cells (ganglionic cells) on a background of connective tissue, together with blood capillaries and sinusoids.

Chromaffin cells

  • LM: large polyhedral cells in groups or cords, surrounded by fenestrated capillaries and sinusoids, with large vesicular nuclei and basophilic cytoplasm.
  • They are innervated by preganglionic sympathetic nerve fibers, so they are considered postsynaptic neurons.
  • Fine cytoplasmic granules stain brown with chromium salts: the chromaffin reaction.
  • EM: prominent Golgi complex, RER, elongated mitochondria, many free ribosomes, and abundant membrane-limited electron-dense granules.

Adrenaline cells

  • Scattered vesicles that are small, homogeneous and less electron dense
  • Secrete epinephrine (adrenaline)

Noradrenaline cells

  • Large vesicles with an electron-dense core
  • Secrete norepinephrine (noradrenaline)

The granules are rich in catecholamines, which act as sympathomimetics.

Ganglion cells

  • The medulla is considered a modified sympathetic ganglion because it contains branched ganglion cells with large vesicular nuclei.
  • They lie between the chromaffin cells.
  • Their axons extend to the parenchyma of the cortex to control its secretory activity.

Paraganglia

Paraganglia are groups of chromaffin cells and nerve cells, similar to those of the medulla, that lie beside the sympathetic ganglia.

Clinical relevance

Paraganglion cells and the cells of the suprarenal medulla may give rise to tumours termed pheochromocytoma.

Blood supply (microcirculation)

Source: page 38
  1. Adrenal arteries

    The superior, middle and inferior adrenal arteries branch and penetrate the capsule at multiple sites.

  2. Subcapsular plexus

    It gives rise to capsular branches, short cortical branches and long cortical branches.

  3. Short cortical branches

    They form a network of sinusoidal capillaries supplying the cortex, then drain into the fenestrated medullary sinusoids.

  4. Long cortical branches

    They descend directly to supply the medulla.

  5. Medullary plexus

    Both short and long cortical branches empty into it, and it ultimately drains into the central vein.

High-yield

The medulla has a dual blood supply: arterial (long cortical branches) and venous from the cortical capillary network (venous portal circulation).

Key terms

Check yourself

Which two cell groups form the parenchyma of the medulla?

Chromaffin cells and nerve (ganglionic) cells.

Where do the short cortical branches end?

They form sinusoidal capillaries supplying the cortex and drain into the fenestrated medullary sinusoids.

What does each cortical zone secrete, from the capsule inwards?

Mineralocorticoids (mostly aldosterone), glucocorticoids, and sex hormones (mainly androgen) with a little glucocorticoid.

Key Points / Quick Review

Stroma

  • Thick capsule covered with renal adipose connective tissue
  • Trabeculae divide the cortex and carry vessels and nerves
  • Reticular fibers support cortex and medulla cells

Cortex

  • Secretions are synthesized and released as needed, not stored
  • Glomerulosa (about 15%): aldosterone
  • Fasciculata (about 80%): glucocorticoids; spongiocytes
  • Reticularis (5 to 7%): androgen; lipofuscin pigments
  • Abundant SER and tubular cristae in all zones

Medulla

  • Neural crest origin; chromaffin and ganglion cells
  • Chromaffin cells are postsynaptic neurons with the chromaffin reaction
  • Adrenaline cells: small vesicles; noradrenaline cells: large dense-core vesicles
  • Paraganglia and medullary cells may give pheochromocytoma

Blood supply

  • Three arteries, then subcapsular plexus
  • Short branches supply the cortex; long branches supply the medulla
  • Medullary plexus drains into the central vein
