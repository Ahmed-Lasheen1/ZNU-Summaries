Wound Management
At a glance

Wounds are classified by skin integrity and by contamination, and each class has its own first aid and repair.

Definition and causes

Source: page 123
Definition

A wound is a loss of continuity of tissues caused by mechanical trauma.

Etiology

  • Blunt trauma: direct compression of tissues.
  • Penetrating trauma: stabbing and firearm injury.

Clinical classification

Source: pages 123 to 124
Wounds
  • Closed wounds skin partly or completely intact
    • Abrasion
    • Contusion
    • Hematoma
  • Open wounds loss of skin continuity
    • Incised cut wound
    • Contused wound

Closed wounds

  • Abrasion: scraping away of the superficial layers of the skin (epidermis) by friction against rough surfaces.
  • Contusion: extravasation of blood in the subcutaneous tissue from injury of small blood vessels by blunt trauma, with intact skin (ecchymosis).
  • Hematoma: extravasation of blood in the deep tissue from injury of a sizable blood vessel.

Possible fates of a hematoma

  • Absorption
  • Encapsulation (blood cyst)
  • Infection
  • Organization (fibrosis)
  • Calcification
  • Formation of a false aneurysm

Open wounds

  • Incised cut wound: made by a sharp object like a razor.
  • Contused wound: made by a hard object forcibly applied over soft tissue.

Therapeutic classification

Source: page 124

Clean wound

  • Presented within 6 hours
  • No gross dirt or devitalized tissue
  • Occurs in clean areas
  • No unclean manipulation

Contaminated wound

  • Presented after 6 hours
  • Contains dirt or devitalized tissue
  • Occurs in contaminated areas (e.g. perineum)
  • Or subjected to unclean manipulation

Infected wound

  • Signs of inflammation
  • Discharges pus

Treatment of open wounds

Source: pages 124 to 125
  • Care of multi-injured patients.
  • Prophylaxis against tetanus and prophylactic antibiotics.
  • Repair depends on the type of wound.
High-yield

Clean wounds are closed at once by primary sutures. Contaminated wounds are left open and the skin is closed later by delayed primary sutures.

Clean wounds

  • Wash the wound with saline and remove any foreign bodies.
  • Deal with all deep structures immediately.
  • Close with primary sutures: direct suturing of clean-cut wounds, or after wound excision in lacerated wounds.

Contaminated wounds

  • Wash with saline or antiseptic solution.
  • Remove all foreign bodies and devitalized tissues.
  • Deal immediately only with important structures, e.g. a bleeding major blood vessel.
  • Leave the wound open with repeated dressing until it becomes clean.
  • Then deal with tendons, nerves and muscles, once sure there is no infection, and close the skin with delayed primary sutures.

Infected wounds

  • Open all pockets of pus.
  • Remove all sutures and necrotic tissues.
  • Wash with antiseptic solution.
  • Repeat dressing until the wound becomes clean.
  • Small gap: secondary sutures. Large gap: skin graft (delayed grafting).

Penetrating wounds

  • Widen the wound and explore the deep structures.
  • Deal with the injured structures.

Treatment of closed wounds

Source: pages 125 to 126
Abrasion
Closed dressing with Vaseline gauze + antibiotic
Contusion
Fomentation: cold in the first 24 hours, then hot in the next days
Small hematoma
Fomentation as above + bandage + antibiotic; usually absorbed
Large hematoma
Aspiration + bandage + antibiotic
Increasing hematoma
Incision + dealing with the bleeder (e.g. ligation)

Check yourself

Source: pages 123 to 126
What are the two etiologies of wounds?

Blunt trauma (direct compression) and penetrating trauma (stabbing, firearm injury).

How does a hematoma differ from a contusion?

A hematoma is blood extravasation in deep tissue from injury of a sizable vessel; a contusion is in subcutaneous tissue from small vessels.

How is the skin of a contaminated wound closed?

The wound is left open with repeated dressing until clean, then the skin is closed by delayed primary sutures.

Key Points / Quick Review

Definition and causes

  • Wound: loss of continuity of tissues by mechanical trauma.
  • Causes: blunt trauma and penetrating trauma.

Clinical classification

  • Closed: abrasion, contusion, hematoma.
  • Open: incised cut wound, contused wound.
  • Hematoma fates: absorption, encapsulation, infection, organization, calcification, false aneurysm.

Therapeutic classification

  • Clean: within 6 hours, no gross dirt.
  • Contaminated: after 6 hours, dirt or devitalized tissue.
  • Infected: inflammation and pus.

Open wound repair

  • Clean: primary sutures.
  • Contaminated: repeated dressing, then delayed primary sutures.
  • Infected: drain pus, then secondary sutures or skin graft.
  • Penetrating: widen and explore.

Closed wound treatment

  • Abrasion: Vaseline gauze + antibiotic.
  • Contusion: cold then hot fomentation.
  • Hematoma: fomentation, aspiration or incision with ligation by size.
