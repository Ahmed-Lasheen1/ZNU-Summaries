The sex cycle and the ovarian cycle

The ovaries have two important interrelated functions: production of gametes (ova) and production of steroid hormones (estrogen and progesterone).

These functions are achieved through the sex (menstrual) cycle. Its length is 28 days, counted from the start of menstrual bleeding to the start of the next. It consists of three cycles:

  • Ovarian cycle
  • Uterine cycle
  • Vaginal cycle

The ovarian cycle is composed of three phases:

Follicular phase

At the start of each cycle, 6 to 12 primordial follicles enlarge to form primary (preantral) follicles. This occurs under the effect of FSH, which is released from the anterior pituitary because the estrogen level is low.

  • The primary oocyte increases in size.
  • Follicular cells proliferate to form granulosa cells around the oocyte.
  • Ovarian stromal cells proliferate to form theca cells around the follicle.

Two-cell mechanism of steroid production

Theca cells

  • Synthesize androgen
  • Under the effect of LH

Granulosa cells

  • Convert androgen to estrogen
  • Under the effect of FSH

Antral (dominant) follicle

After one week (at the 7th day of the cycle), the largest follicle in one ovary enlarges, becomes highly developed and secretes more estrogen. It is called the antral follicle or dominant follicle. The other (competing) follicles regress and degenerate.

  • It contains a large cavity filled with fluid, the antrum: the small spaces unite to give one large space.
  • The oocyte is located in a mass of granulosa cells (cumulus oophorus) that projects into the antrum.
  • The first layer of granulosa cells around the oocyte, in close contact with the zona pellucida, becomes elongated and is called the corona radiata.
  • In the presence of FSH and increased estrogens, the outer layers of granulosa cells of the large antral follicle synthesize LH receptors.

Mechanism of uniovulation

The dominant follicle secretes a large amount of estrogen, which acts in two directions. On the competing follicles, it gives negative feedback:

Estrogen
FSH secretion
Growth of competing follicles

The competing follicles therefore become atretic. On the dominant follicle itself, estrogen gives positive feedback:

  • Increased estrogen inside the follicle increases the number and sensitivity of FSH receptors on its granulosa cells.
  • The granulosa cells respond more to FSH, so estrogen secretion increases and the follicle grows more.
  • The dominant follicle also secretes inhibitory peptides that cause degeneration of the competing follicles.

Just before ovulation

The follicle becomes a mature graafian follicle (1 to 2 cm in diameter). The oocyte starts the reduction division of meiosis to produce a secondary oocyte and the first polar body. The secondary oocyte is arrested in metaphase until fertilization.

Ovulation

Definition

Ovulation is the rupture of the mature graafian follicle and expulsion of the ovum into the peritoneal cavity, to be picked up by the fimbriated end of the fallopian tube. It occurs on the 14th day of the cycle (mid-cyclic).

Before ovulation

Premature oocyte maturation and luteinization are prevented by two inhibitors, both formed by granulosa cells and both removed by the pre-ovulatory LH surge.

Oocyte maturation inhibitor (OMI)

  • Formed by granulosa cells
  • Prevents meiosis of the oocyte

Luteinization inhibitor (LI)

  • Formed by granulosa cells
  • Inhibits luteinization

The gonadotropin surge

The gonadotropin surge is a mid-cyclic increase in the blood level of gonadotropins (LH and FSH). It is the cause of ovulation. When the estrogen level reaches a high critical level and is maintained for a critical time, it induces a sudden increase in LH (the LH surge).

High-yield

Aim of the surge: final maturation of the oocyte (removal of the inhibitory effect of OMI) and physical release of the oocyte from the mature follicle (removal of the inhibitory effect of LI).

Functions of the LH surge

  • Completes meiosis (removes OMI inhibition)
  • Luteinization of granulosa cells (removes LI inhibition)
  • Increased progesterone synthesis: hyperemia, plasma transudation and swelling of the follicle; increased distensibility of the follicular wall so it accommodates the rapid rise in fluid without a change in intrafollicular pressure
  • Increased collagenase activity: digestion of collagen in the follicular wall
  • Increased plasminogen activator: converts plasminogen to plasmin, which lyses the follicular wall connective tissue
  • Increased prostaglandins (PGE and PGF): free lysosomal enzymes within the follicular fluid to digest the wall, and contract the ovarian smooth muscles to help expel the ovum into the peritoneal cavity

Functions of the FSH surge

  • Increases the number of LH receptors on granulosa cells, for luteinization and normal corpus luteum formation
  • Stimulates granulosa cells to produce plasminogen activator

Signs of ovulation

Ovulation and corpus luteum formation are indicated by signs of progesterone release. If the cycle is regular, ovulation is 14 days from the start of menstruation.

Failure of ovulation (anovulatory cycles)

  • Present normally in the first 1 to 2 years after the onset of puberty and before menopause.
  • Bleeding is due to withdrawal of estrogen, because of atrophy of the graafian follicle.
  • Occur also in women treated with contraceptive pills.
Clinical relevance

Some women are infertile because of anovulatory cycles.

Luteal phase and corpus luteum

The luteal phase lasts 14 days, is under the control of LH and is characterized by secretion of a large amount of progesterone.

Formation of the corpus luteum

  1. Corpus hemorrhagicum

    After ovulation, the ruptured follicle becomes filled with blood.

  2. Clearing of the clot

    The clotted blood is removed by phagocytosis.

  3. Conversion under LH

    The structure is converted to the corpus luteum under the effect of LH.

  4. Lutein cells

    Both granulosa and theca cells are converted to lutein cells, which are rich in lipids, so the corpus luteum is yellow.

Function

The corpus luteum is a temporary endocrine organ that secretes progesterone and estrogen. About 8 days after ovulation there is a progesterone peak with a second peak of estrogen (the first estrogen peak is mid-cyclic).

Fate of the corpus luteum

No fertilization and no implantation

  • LH falls because of the high progesterone and estrogen levels
  • Corpus luteum degenerates (luteolysis): progesterone and estrogen drop 9 days after ovulation
  • After 14 days it becomes the corpus albicans, removed by macrophages leaving a fibrous scar
  • The drop causes shedding of the endometrium (menstruation) and stimulation of FSH secretion to start another cycle

Fertilization occurs

  • Corpus luteum is maintained by human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) for 6 months
  • It is then called the corpus luteum of pregnancy
  • It secretes progesterone for stabilization of pregnancy

Uterine cycle

Definition

The uterine cycle is the cyclic change of the endometrium under the effect of ovarian hormones. Its aim is to prepare the endometrium to receive the zygote (implantation). If there is no implantation, the prepared endometrium is destroyed as menstruation.

PhaseTimingHormonal basis
Menstrual (destructive) phase1st to 5th dayDegeneration of the corpus luteum removes the hormonal support of the endometrium
Proliferative phase5th to 14th dayEstrogen from the developing follicle; corresponds to the follicular phase in the ovary
Secretory phase15th day to the beginning of the next cycleProgesterone and estrogen from the corpus luteum; corresponds to the luteal phase in the ovary

Mechanism of menstruation

  1. Loss of hormonal support

    The corpus luteum degenerates, so the hormonal support of the endometrium is removed.

  2. Vasoconstriction

    Vasoconstriction of the spiral arteries causes ischemia and necrosis of the functional layer.

  3. Vasodilatation

    Vasodilatation of the spiral arteries then causes rupture and hemorrhage.

  4. Shedding

    The endometrium is shed in patches soaked with blood.

  5. End of bleeding

    Bleeding ends when the spiral arteries constrict again.

Menstrual blood is not clotted because of the fibrinolytic system.

Key terms

Check yourself

Why does only one follicle usually complete its growth in each cycle?

The dominant follicle's estrogen lowers FSH (negative feedback on competing follicles, which become atretic) while increasing FSH receptors on its own granulosa cells.

What induces the LH surge, and what does the surge achieve?

A high critical estrogen level maintained for a critical time induces it. It matures the oocyte (OMI removed) and releases it from the follicle (LI removed).

What happens to the spiral arteries during menstruation?

They vasoconstrict (ischemia and necrosis), then vasodilate (rupture and hemorrhage), and bleeding ends when they constrict again.

What happens to the corpus luteum if fertilization does not occur?

LH falls, the corpus luteum degenerates (luteolysis) and becomes the corpus albicans, and the drop in progesterone and estrogen causes menstruation.

Key Points / Quick Review

Sex cycle and follicular phase

  • The sex cycle lasts 28 days: ovarian, uterine and vaginal cycles.
  • Ovarian cycle: follicular (FSH, estrogen), ovulation (LH and FSH surge), luteal (LH, progesterone).
  • Theca cells make androgen under LH; granulosa cells convert it to estrogen under FSH.
  • The dominant follicle appears at day 7 and suppresses the competing follicles (uniovulation).

Ovulation

  • Occurs on day 14; OMI and LI from granulosa cells are removed by the LH surge.
  • LH surge: meiosis completed, luteinization, progesterone, collagenase, plasminogen activator, prostaglandins.
  • FSH surge: more LH receptors on granulosa cells and plasminogen activator.
  • Signs: pregnanediol in urine, temperature rise of 0.5 °C, early secretory endometrium.
  • Anovulatory cycles are normal in the first 1 to 2 years after puberty and before menopause.

Luteal phase

  • Corpus hemorrhagicum becomes the corpus luteum under LH; lutein cells are lipid-rich, so it is yellow.
  • The corpus luteum secretes progesterone and estrogen.
  • No fertilization: luteolysis, then corpus albicans after 14 days.
  • Fertilization: hCG maintains it as the corpus luteum of pregnancy.

Uterine cycle

  • Aim: prepare the endometrium for implantation.
  • Menstrual phase: days 1 to 5, after corpus luteum degeneration.
  • Proliferative phase (days 5 to 14): estrogen; secretory phase (day 15 onward): progesterone and estrogen.
  • Menstrual blood is not clotted because of the fibrinolytic system.
