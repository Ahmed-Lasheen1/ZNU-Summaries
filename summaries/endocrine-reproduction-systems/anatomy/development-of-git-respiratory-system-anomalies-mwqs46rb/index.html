Formation of the gut

Source: page 107

In shortFolding of the embryo traps part of the yolk sac inside the body to make the gut.

  • Development of the gut begins at the 4th week with folding of the embryo.
  • During folding, part of the yolk sac is incorporated inside the embryo and forms the gut.
  • The remaining part bulges outside the body and forms the definitive yolk sac, connected to the midgut by the vitello-intestinal duct.

High-yield

The duodenum and the pancreas each have a dual origin: the upper half of the duodenum and most of the pancreas come from the foregut, while the lower half of the duodenum and the lower half of the pancreatic head come from the midgut.

Development of the anal canal

Source: page 109

Upper half

  • Derived from the hindgut
  • Endodermal

Lower half

  • Derived from an ectodermal depression, the proctodeum (anal pit)
  • Ectodermal
  • The roof of the proctodeum is the anal membrane, which separates the upper (endodermal) half of the anal canal from the lower (ectodermal) half.
  • Normally the anal membrane degenerates at the 7th week of intrauterine life.
Clinical relevance

Failure of the anal membrane to rupture leads to imperforate anus.

Respiratory diverticulum

Source: page 110

In shortThe respiratory system is endodermal and buds off the ventral wall of the foregut.

The respiratory system is endodermal in origin. It develops from a median diverticulum of the foregut called the respiratory diverticulum, so the lining epithelium of the larynx, trachea, bronchi and lungs is derived from endoderm.

  1. Outgrowth

    The respiratory diverticulum grows out from the ventral wall of the foregut.

  2. Separation

    Its distal part is separated from the esophagus by the development of the tracheoesophageal septum.

  3. Pharyngeal communication

    Its cranial part continues to communicate with the pharynx; this communication forms the laryngeal inlet.

Tracheoesophageal fistula (TEF)

Source: pages 110 to 112
Definition

An abnormal communication between the trachea and the esophagus. It is often associated with esophageal atresia and occurs in 1 in 3000 to 4500 births.

Cause

TEF occurs due to defective development of the tracheoesophageal septum: the tracheoesophageal folds fail to fuse with each other in a small segment, leaving an abnormal communication.

Types

TypeArrangement
(a)Upper esophagus ends in a blind pouch; lower part communicates with the trachea (85 to 90%)
(b)As (a), but the tracheoesophageal communication is replaced by a fibrous cord
(c)Both upper and lower parts communicate with the trachea by a common narrow canal: H-shaped TEF (4%)
(d)Upper part communicates with the trachea; lower end forms a blind pouch
(e)Upper and lower parts each communicate with the trachea separately
Clinical relevance

When milk or fluid is given to a newborn with TEF, coughing and choking occur because the fluid enters the respiratory tract. This may also lead to lung infection (pneumonia).

Key Points / Quick Review

Gut formation

  • Starts at week 4 with embryonic folding; part of the yolk sac becomes the gut.
  • Foregut in the head fold, hindgut in the tail fold, midgut between.
  • Midgut connects to the definitive yolk sac by the vitello-intestinal duct.

Derivatives

  • Foregut: pharynx, respiratory bud, esophagus, stomach, upper duodenum, biliary system, upper pancreas.
  • Midgut: lower duodenum, jejunum, ileum, caecum, ascending colon, right two-thirds of transverse colon.
  • Hindgut: left third of transverse colon to rectum, and the upper half of the anal canal.

Anal canal

  • Lower half arises from the proctodeum; anal membrane separates the halves.
  • Membrane ruptures at week 7; failure gives imperforate anus.

Respiratory system and TEF

  • Endodermal; arises from the respiratory diverticulum on the ventral foregut wall.
  • Tracheoesophageal septum separates the trachea from the esophagus.
  • TEF: defective septum, often with esophageal atresia; type (a) is the commonest (85 to 90%).
  • Newborns cough and choke on feeding and may develop pneumonia.
