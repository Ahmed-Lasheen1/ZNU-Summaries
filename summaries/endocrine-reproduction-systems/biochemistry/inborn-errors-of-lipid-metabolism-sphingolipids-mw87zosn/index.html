Biosynthesis of glycerol-containing phospholipids

Source: page 147

All mammalian phospholipids start from phosphatidic acid, the parent of the phospholipids.

  1. Glycerol 3-phosphate

    Biosynthesis of glycerol 3-P.

  2. Phosphatidic acid

    The parent for phospholipids.

  3. Diacylglycerol

    Phosphatidic acid is converted into diacylglycerol.

  4. Nitrogenous base added

    A nitrogenous base is added to the diacylglycerol.

Phosphatidic acid is contributed to either diacylglycerol or CDP-diacylglycerol. The route taken decides which phospholipid is formed:

CDP-choline + diacylglycerol
Phosphatidylcholine (choline is first activated to CDP-choline)
Inositol + CDP-diacylglycerol
Phosphatidylinositol
Serine + CDP-diacylglycerol
Phosphatidylserine
Decarboxylation of phosphatidylserine
Phosphatidylethanolamine

Sphingolipids: structure and biosynthesis

Source: page 148

Sphingolipids are important constituents of cell membranes and contain a sphingosine base. They have a hydrophilic region and two fatty acid-derived hydrophobic tails.

Definition

Ceramide = sphingosine base + fatty acid. It is the parent of the sphingolipids and is considered the active form of the sphingosine base.

Classes

  • Sphingomyelin
  • Cerebrosides
  • Gangliosides

Biosynthesis

  1. Sphingosine base

    Made from serine + palmitoyl CoA.

  2. Ceramide

    Sphingosine base + fatty acid.

  3. Sphingomyelin

    Ceramide + CDP-choline.

  4. Cerebrosides

    Ceramide + UDP-galactose.

  5. Gangliosides

    Ceramide + oligosaccharides + sialic acid.

Degradation of phospholipids and sphingolipids

Source: page 149

Phospholipids

  • Catabolized by phospholipases
  • PLA1, PLA2 and phospholipase C (PLC)

Sphingolipids

  • Sphingomyelinase
  • Glucocerebrosidase
  • Hexosaminidase

Lipid storage diseases (sphingolipidosis)

Source: pages 149 to 150

Sphingolipidoses are a subcategory of lysosomal storage disorders. Deficient lysosomal degradation or transport of sphingolipids makes them accumulate in a single organ or in multiple organs.

High-yield

All sphingolipidoses present with neurological manifestations. Some also present with visceral and skeletal manifestations.

Check yourself

What is ceramide made of?

A sphingosine base plus a fatty acid.

Which enzyme is deficient in Gaucher disease?

Glucocerebrosidase, so glucocerebroside accumulates.

Which phospholipid is formed by decarboxylation of phosphatidylserine?

Phosphatidylethanolamine.

Key Points / Quick Review

Phospholipids

  • All start from phosphatidic acid (via diacylglycerol or CDP-diacylglycerol).
  • CDP-choline + diacylglycerol gives phosphatidylcholine.
  • Inositol or serine + CDP-diacylglycerol gives phosphatidylinositol or phosphatidylserine; decarboxylation of phosphatidylserine gives phosphatidylethanolamine.
  • Broken down by phospholipases (PLA1, PLA2, PLC).

Sphingolipids

  • Ceramide (sphingosine base + fatty acid) is the parent.
  • Sphingosine base = serine + palmitoyl CoA.
  • Sphingomyelin = ceramide + CDP-choline; cerebrosides = ceramide + UDP-galactose; gangliosides = ceramide + oligosaccharides + sialic acid.
  • Broken down by sphingomyelinase, glucocerebrosidase and hexosaminidase.

Sphingolipidoses

  • Lysosomal storage disorders; all have neurological manifestations.
  • Tay-Sachs: hexosaminidase A, ganglioside.
  • Gaucher: glucocerebrosidase, glucocerebroside.
  • Niemann-Pick: sphingomyelinase, sphingomyelin.
  • Management: enzyme replacement, substrate reduction, chemical chaperone, gene therapy, bone marrow transplantation.
