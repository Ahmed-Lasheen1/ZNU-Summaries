Shape, position and origin

The placenta is a discoid (disc-like) organ with a double origin: fetal and maternal.

  • Diameter: 15-25 cm
  • Weight: 500 g (half a kilogram)
  • Thickness: 3 cm at its center, tapering gradually toward the periphery
  • Position: upper part of the posterior wall of the uterus

Fetal origin

  • Chorion frondosum

Maternal origin

  • Decidua basalis

Decidua and chorion

Definition

The decidua is the endometrium of the pregnant uterus after implantation.

Parts of the decidua (in relation to the blastocyst)

Decidua capsularis
Between the blastocyst and the uterine cavity
Decidua basalis
Between the blastocyst and the wall of the uterus
Decidua parietalis
Lines the remaining wall of the uterus

The chorion

Finger-like chorionic villi develop from the syncytiotrophoblast and surround the chorionic vesicle. According to their relation to the decidua, they are of two types.

Chorion frondosum

  • Lies between the embryo and the decidua basalis
  • Villi become thick and keep growing
  • Forms the fetal part of the placenta

Chorion laeve

  • Lies between the embryo and the decidua capsularis
  • Villi are smooth
  • Degenerates at the third month

Types of chorionic villi

TypeTimeStructure
Primary villiDay 11-13Syncytiotrophoblast cover with a cytotrophoblast core; finger-like protrusions into the decidua basalis
Secondary villiDay 16Extraembryonic mesoderm invades the core of the primary villi
Tertiary villiDay 21Extraembryonic vessels appear within the mesodermal core

Later, chorionic arteries and veins connect with the tertiary villi and the intraembryonic circulation, linking the placenta and the embryo. Maternal blood baths these villi.

Stem (anchoring) villi: the cytotrophoblast core proliferates through the syncytiotrophoblast and reaches the maternal endometrium, making contact with similar extensions of neighboring villous stems. Together they form a thin outer cytotrophoblast shell.

Internal structure

  • Chorionic villi extend from the chorionic plate to the decidua basalis, floating in maternal blood in the intervillous space.
  • Intervillous spaces lie between the stem villi and are filled with maternal blood.
  • Cytotrophoblast shell: a complete sheet of cytotrophoblastic cells between the decidua basalis and the intervillous spaces.
  • Placental septa: during the fourth and fifth months the decidua forms decidual septa that project into the intervillous spaces and divide the placenta into 15-25 compartments (cotyledons).

Placental barrier

The placental barrier separates maternal and fetal blood. Early in pregnancy it has four layers:

  1. Syncytiotrophoblast
  2. Cytotrophoblast
  3. Primary mesoderm
  4. Endothelial lining of fetal blood vessels
High-yield

In the second half of pregnancy the barrier becomes thinner because the cytotrophoblast and primary mesoderm degenerate. It prevents mixing of maternal and fetal blood.

Surfaces of the placenta

Fetal surface

  • Smooth, shiny and glistening
  • Covered by amnion, which is reflected on the cord
  • Cord inserted near or at the center
  • Radiating branches of the cord vessels seen beneath the amnion

Maternal surface

  • Rough, irregular, dull greyish red
  • Divided into 15-25 cotyledons
  • Each cotyledon: branches of one main villus stem covered by decidua basalis

Functions of the placenta

  1. Respiratory function
  2. Nutritive function
  3. Excretory function
  4. Production of enzymes
  5. Production of pregnancy-associated plasma proteins (PAPP)
  6. Barrier function
  7. Endocrine function

Abnormalities of the placenta

Abnormal shape

Bilobed (bipartite)
Two equal lobes connected by placental tissue
Succenturiate
A large lobe and a smaller one connected by membranes
Circumvallate
Whitish ring of decidua around the placenta, seen from the fetal surface
Fenestrate
An abnormal opening in the placenta
Membranous
Consists of membranous placental tissue

Abnormal size

  • Very small placenta: found in women with chronic hypertension.
  • Very large placenta: found in fetal hydrops, a fetal condition of severe hemolytic disease from serological incompatibility between mother and fetus.

Abnormal site

Definition

Placenta previa: the placenta is partly or totally attached to the lower uterine segment.

Abnormal attachment of the cord

Marginal (battledore)

  • Cord attached to the margin of the placenta

Velamentous

  • Umbilical vessels attach to the amnion and ramify there before reaching the placenta

Abnormal adhesion

Placenta accreta
Villi reach the myometrium, due to deficient decidua basalis
Placenta increta
Villi penetrate deeply into the myometrium
Placenta percreta
Villi reach the peritoneal coat

Check yourself

Which two tissues give rise to the placenta?

Chorion frondosum (fetal) and decidua basalis (maternal).

What fills the intervillous spaces, and what divides the placenta into cotyledons?

Maternal blood fills them; decidual septa divide the placenta into 15-25 cotyledons.

Which adhesion abnormality has villi reaching the peritoneal coat?

Placenta percreta.

Key Points / Quick Review

Origin and form

  • Discoid, 15-25 cm, 500 g, 3 cm thick at the center.
  • Chorion frondosum (fetal) plus decidua basalis (maternal).
  • Chorion laeve is smooth and degenerates at the third month.

Villi

  • Primary day 11-13, secondary day 16, tertiary day 21.
  • Stem villi form the cytotrophoblast shell.
  • Villi float in maternal blood in the intervillous space.

Structure

  • Early barrier: four layers; thins in the second half of pregnancy.
  • Decidual septa divide the placenta into 15-25 cotyledons.
  • Fetal surface smooth and shiny; maternal surface rough.

Abnormalities

  • Shape: bilobed, succenturiate, circumvallate, fenestrate, membranous.
  • Previa: lower uterine segment.
  • Cord: marginal or velamentous.
  • Adhesion: accreta, increta, percreta.
