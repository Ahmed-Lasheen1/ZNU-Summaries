Facial Nerve
At a glance

The facial nerve is a mixed nerve with motor, sensory and parasympathetic fibers; this lesson covers its nuclei, its course through the facial canal and its two key branches in the canal.

Nuclei and fiber components

The facial nerve has five nuclei and carries mixed types of fibers.

Exit from the brainstem and course

In shortThe nerve leaves the brainstem between the pons and the olive, runs through the facial canal past the geniculate ganglion, and exits at the stylomastoid foramen.

The facial nerve emerges from the brainstem in the groove between the pons and the olive of the medulla oblongata. The course below is that of its intracranial part.

  1. Enter the facial canal

    The nerve passes through the internal acoustic meatus to enter the facial canal. It first runs forwards and laterally to reach the medial wall of the middle ear.

  2. Curve over the promontory

    It then curves above the medial wall of the middle ear to reach the promontory.

  3. Form the geniculate ganglion

    The sharp curve resembles a knee (geniculum) and forms the geniculate ganglion.

  4. Descend and exit

    The nerve descends vertically behind the posterior wall of the middle ear and emerges from the stylomastoid foramen.

The branches of the extracranial part are described with the face.

Branches within the facial canal

The intracranial part gives three branches inside the facial canal:

  1. Greater superficial petrosal nerve
  2. Motor branch to the stapedius muscle
  3. Chorda tympani nerve

Greater superficial petrosal nerve

This nerve arises from the facial nerve at the geniculate ganglion within the facial canal, then emerges from the facial canal and the temporal bone. It unites with the deep petrosal nerve to form the nerve of the pterygoid canal (Vidian nerve).

Pterygopalatine ganglion

The Vidian nerve passes through the pterygoid canal to enter the pterygopalatine fossa and relays in the pterygopalatine ganglion.

What it carries

  • Preganglionic parasympathetic fibers to the lacrimal gland and to the glands of the nose, palate and nasopharynx.
  • Taste sensation from the palate.

Chorda tympani nerve

The chorda tympani takes origin from the facial nerve just above the stylomastoid foramen. It enters the middle ear and passes forwards between the mucous membrane and the fibrous layer of the tympanic membrane. It then exits to the infratemporal fossa and joins the lingual nerve at an acute angle, deep to the lateral pterygoid muscle.

Check yourself

Which nucleus gives the parasympathetic fibers of the facial nerve, and where is it?

The superior salivary nucleus, in the pons. It supplies the lacrimal, nasal and palatine glands and the submandibular and sublingual salivary glands.

What forms the geniculate ganglion?

The sharp curve of the nerve above the medial wall of the middle ear at the promontory, which resembles a knee (geniculum).

Which two nerves unite to form the nerve of the pterygoid canal?

The greater superficial petrosal nerve and the deep petrosal nerve.

Where does the chorda tympani join the lingual nerve?

At an acute angle, deep to the lateral pterygoid muscle in the infratemporal fossa.

Key Points / Quick Review

Nuclei

  • Five nuclei: motor, superior salivary, spinal nucleus of trigeminal, solitary, mesencephalic.
  • Motor nucleus (pons): muscles of the face, posterior belly of digastric, stylohyoid.
  • Superior salivary nucleus (pons): parasympathetic to lacrimal, nasal, palatine, submandibular and sublingual glands.
  • Solitary nucleus: taste from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue.
  • Mesencephalic nucleus: unconscious proprioception.

Course

  • Emerges between the pons and the olive of the medulla.
  • Internal acoustic meatus, then facial canal, then medial wall of the middle ear.
  • Geniculate ganglion forms at the sharp curve over the promontory.
  • Descends behind the posterior wall of the middle ear and exits at the stylomastoid foramen.

Branches in the facial canal

  • Greater superficial petrosal nerve.
  • Motor branch to stapedius.
  • Chorda tympani.

Greater superficial petrosal nerve

  • Arises at the geniculate ganglion.
  • Unites with the deep petrosal nerve to form the Vidian nerve, which relays in the pterygopalatine ganglion.
  • Carries parasympathetic fibers to the lacrimal gland and to the glands of the nose, palate and nasopharynx, and taste from the palate.

Chorda tympani

  • Arises just above the stylomastoid foramen.
  • Joins the lingual nerve deep to the lateral pterygoid.
  • Parasympathetic fibers to the submandibular and sublingual glands (relay in the submandibular ganglion) and taste from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue.
