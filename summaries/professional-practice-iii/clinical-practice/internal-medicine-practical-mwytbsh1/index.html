Station 1

  1. Name the four injection techniques shown.
    • Intramuscular (IM)
    • Subcutaneous (SC)
    • Intravenous (IV)
    • Intradermal (ID)
  2. State the angle of needle insertion for an intravenous injection.

    15-30 degrees with the bevel up.

Station 2

  1. Enumerate the steps of an intravenous injection.
    • Introduce yourself, explain what you would like to do and obtain verbal consent.
    • Determine the injection site: choose a large, visible vein (e.g., median cubital); avoid inflamed or hard veins; rub the site with a swab to disinfect; wear sterile gloves.
    • Apply a tourniquet to engorge the veins and make them more prominent.
    • Needle insertion: insert the needle at a 15-30 degree angle with the bevel up; once blood is aspirated, advance the catheter into the vein.
    • Confirm placement by aspirating blood into the catheter.
    • Secure the catheter: remove the needle while holding the catheter in place, secure with adhesive tape and apply a sterile dressing.

Station 3

  1. Enumerate the indications of IV injection.
    • Dehydration (loss of body fluids without adequate replacement)
    • Malnutrition (nutrient deficiencies)
    • Cancer (to administer chemotherapy)
    • Autoimmune disorders (the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own tissues)
    • Infections and other diseases

Station 4

  1. Enumerate the complications of IV injection.
    • Burning, stinging or redness if the IV solution leaks onto the skin around the injection site
    • Pulmonary edema (fluid overload): shortness of breath and coughing up a frothy substance
    • Air embolism (air enters the vein): shortness of breath, increased heart rate, shoulder pain, light-headedness and confusion
    • Infections and other diseases

Station 5

  1. What are the types of medication administration by IV injection?
    • Bolus injection: push the medication into the vein rapidly as prescribed.
    • Infusion: connect the IV line to the catheter and set the infusion rate according to the physician's order.
  2. What is the aftercare of IV injection?
    • Site monitoring: check for redness, swelling or discomfort at the insertion site; observe the patient for immediate reactions to the medication.
    • Documentation: record the medication administered, dose, time and any observations or complications.
    • Disposal: dispose of needles and sharps in a designated sharps container; clean up the area and equipment.

Station 6

  1. Enumerate the steps of canula insertion.
    • Introduce yourself, explain what you would like to do and obtain verbal consent.
    • Apply the tourniquet above the intended site to distend the vein.
    • Palpate the vein: identify a suitable vein by palpation or visualization; clean the site with antiseptic solution.
    • Insert the cannula at a 15-30 degree angle with a steady motion.
    • Advance the cannula further once blood returns in the flashback chamber.
    • Secure the cannula: release the tourniquet and secure with a sterile dressing.
    • Flush the cannula with normal saline to ensure patency and correct placement.

Station 7

  1. What veins should you avoid?

    Veins that are inflamed or hard.

  2. Which vein is chosen for an IV injection?

    A large, visible vein, e.g., the median cubital vein.

  3. Where is the tourniquet placed for cannulation, and why?

    Above the intended site, to distend the vein.

  4. What is used to flush the cannula?

    Normal saline, to ensure patency and correct placement.

Station 8

  1. Identify the oxygen delivery device shown.

    Nasal cannula

  2. Describe this device.

    Lightweight device with two prongs that sit in the nostrils.

  3. State its oxygen flow rate and approximate FiO2.

    1-6 L/min, approximate FiO2 of 24-44%.

  4. State its usage.

    Patients with mild hypoxemia who require a low to moderate amount of supplemental oxygen.

Station 9

  1. Identify the oxygen delivery device shown.

    Simple face mask

  2. Describe this device.

    Mask covering the nose and mouth with side vents to allow exhaled air to escape.

  3. State its oxygen flow rate and approximate FiO2.

    5-10 L/min, approximate FiO2 of 40-60%.

  4. State its usage.

    Patients requiring moderate to high flow rates.

Station 10

  1. Identify the oxygen delivery device shown.

    Partial rebreather mask

  2. Describe this device.

    Similar to the simple mask but with a reservoir bag to capture exhaled air.

  3. State its oxygen flow rate and approximate FiO2.

    6-10 L/min, approximate FiO2 of 60-80%.

  4. State its usage.

    Short-term therapy in severe hypoxemia.

Station 11

  1. Identify the oxygen delivery device shown.

    Non-rebreather mask

  2. Describe this device.

    Includes a reservoir bag and one-way valves to prevent exhaled air from entering the bag.

  3. State its oxygen flow rate and approximate FiO2.

    10-15 L/min, approximate FiO2 of 80-100%.

  4. State its usage.

    Critical patients needing the highest concentration of oxygen.

Station 12

  1. Arrange the four oxygen delivery devices by flow rate, lowest to highest, with FiO2.
    • Nasal cannula: 1-6 L/min, FiO2 24-44%
    • Simple face mask: 5-10 L/min, FiO2 40-60%
    • Partial rebreather mask: 6-10 L/min, FiO2 60-80%
    • Non-rebreather mask: 10-15 L/min, FiO2 80-100%

Station 13

  1. Enumerate the indications for oxygen therapy.
    • Acute: hypoxemia (low blood oxygen, measured by pulse oximetry or arterial blood gas analysis); respiratory distress (pneumonia, COPD exacerbations, acute asthma attacks); cardiac conditions (acute myocardial infarction, heart failure).
    • Chronic: COPD (long-term oxygen to manage chronic hypoxemia); interstitial lung disease (continuous oxygen support); sleep apnea (home oxygen for significant hypoxemia during sleep).
    • Special populations: premature infants; patients with cystic fibrosis (chronic lung infections and inflammation).

Station 14

  1. Enumerate the complications of oxygen therapy with their symptoms and management.
    • Oxygen toxicity: chest pain, cough, visual disturbances. Reduce FiO2 to the lowest effective level and monitor for symptoms.
    • Barotrauma: pneumothorax or pneumomediastinum. Adjust pressure settings, monitor for signs, seek medical intervention if necessary.
    • Absorption atelectasis: collapse of lung tissue leading to reduced gas exchange. Ensure appropriate FiO2 levels, monitor for signs, use techniques to promote lung expansion.
    • Dryness and discomfort: dry nasal passages or throat irritation. Use a humidifier with oxygen therapy.
