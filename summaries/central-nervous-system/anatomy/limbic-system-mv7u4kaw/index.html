Limbic System
At a glance

The limbic system is a group of cortical gyri, nuclei and fiber tracts bordering the brainstem, with the hippocampus and its fornix outflow as its core pathway.

Location and parts

The limbic system lies lateral to the thalamus, above the brainstem and underneath the cerebral cortex.

Limbic means border: its structures sit on the borders around the brainstem and beneath the cortex.

Cortical gyri (limbic lobe)

  • Orbital frontal lobe
  • Cingulate gyrus
  • Isthmus
  • Hippocampal formation: parahippocampus, hippocampus and dentate gyrus

Other components

  • Mamillary body
  • Anterior nucleus of the thalamus
  • Anterior perforated substance
  • Septal nuclei
  • Amygdaloid nucleus

Fibers and tracts

  • Fimbria and fornix

Hippocampus and its outflow

The hippocampus is a coiled mass of gray matter embedded along the edge of the temporal lobe. In cross-section it has the shape of a seahorse, which is what the name means.

From hippocampus to mamillary body

  1. Alveus

    The fibers of the hippocampal cells form a white matter sheet called the alveus.

  2. Fimbria

    The alveus continues as the fimbria, which projects upward to form the crus of the fornix.

  3. Fornix

    The two crura fuse below the splenium of the corpus callosum to form one band, the fornix, which passes below the corpus callosum.

  4. Columns to mamillary bodies

    In front of the anterior perforated substance the fornix gives two rounded columns, each curving downward to end in the mamillary bodies.

Some fibers from the crus of the fornix cross to the opposite hippocampus, forming the hippocampal commissure (commissure of the fornix).

Dentate gyrus

The dentate gyrus is a band of gray matter between the fimbria and the parahippocampus. It is considered a modified part of the hippocampus.

Check yourself

Which three structures make up the hippocampal formation?

The parahippocampus, the hippocampus and the dentate gyrus.

Where do the two crura of the fornix fuse, and where do the fornix columns end?

The crura fuse below the splenium of the corpus callosum. The columns end in the mamillary bodies.

What is the dentate gyrus, and where does it lie?

A modified part of the hippocampus, a band of gray matter between the fimbria and the parahippocampus.

Key Points / Quick Review

Location

  • Lateral to the thalamus, above the brainstem, beneath the cerebral cortex.
  • "Limbic" = border.

Parts

  • Limbic lobe: orbital frontal lobe, cingulate gyrus, isthmus, hippocampal formation.
  • Other: mamillary body, anterior thalamic nucleus, anterior perforated substance, septal nuclei, amygdaloid nucleus.
  • Tracts: fimbria and fornix.

Hippocampal pathway

  • Seahorse-shaped gray matter along the temporal lobe edge.
  • Alveus, fimbria, crus of fornix, fornix, columns, mamillary bodies.
  • Hippocampal commissure: crus fibers crossing to the opposite hippocampus.

Dentate gyrus

  • Between the fimbria and parahippocampus; a modified part of the hippocampus.
