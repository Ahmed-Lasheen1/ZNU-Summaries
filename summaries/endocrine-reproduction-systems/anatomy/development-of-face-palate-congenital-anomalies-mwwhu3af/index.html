Facial processes

Source: pages 153 to 154

At the end of the 4th week, five mesenchymal masses appear around the stomodeum (the future mouth cavity).

  • Frontonasal process: one, cranial; develops from mesoderm on the under surface of the brain.
  • Maxillary processes: two, one on each side of the stomodeum; from the mesoderm of the 1st pharyngeal arch.
  • Mandibular processes: two, one on each side below the stomodeum; from the mesoderm of the 1st pharyngeal arch.

Nasal placode and nasal pit

  • An ectodermal thickening, the nasal placode, appears on the frontonasal process, one on each side just above the stomodeum.
  • The floor of each placode is depressed to form a nasal pit.
  • The nasal pit divides the frontonasal process into three parts: two lateral nasal processes (right and left) and one median nasal process.
  • The lower lateral edge of the median nasal process is concave and is called the globular process.

Fusion of the processes

Source: pages 154 to 155

Order of fusion

  • The mandibular processes grow first, are directed medially and fuse in the midline below the stomodeum to form the primitive lower jaw.
  • Two weeks later the maxillary processes enlarge, grow medially and compress the median nasal processes toward the midline.
  • Once the maxillary processes contact them, the globular processes of the median nasal processes fuse with each other in the midline to form the primitive palate.
  • The maxillary processes fuse with the lateral nasal process and keep growing medially: they cover the median nasal process and fuse with each other in the midline above the stomodeum to form the definitive upper jaw.

Nasolacrimal duct and lacrimal sac

The maxillary process and the lateral nasal process are separated by a deep nasolacrimal groove.

Nasolacrimal duct
  • The cord forms from ectoderm on the floor of the groove and extends from the medial corner of the eye to the inferior meatus of the nose.
  • The upper end of the duct widens to form the lacrimal sac; the maxillary process then grows and covers the lateral nasal process and the duct.

Development of the palate

Source: page 155

Primitive palate

Formed by union of the globular processes of the median nasal prominences in the midline. In the adult it gives:

  • The philtrum of the upper lip.
  • The part of the upper jaw that carries the upper incisor teeth.
  • A palatal part lying in front of the incisive fossa.

Definitive palate

  • In the 6th week, two shelf-like palatine shelves grow from the inner sides of the maxillary processes.
  • In the 7th week the shelves fuse in the midline above the tongue to form the secondary palate.
  • They also fuse anteriorly with the primary palate, from which they are separated by the incisive foramen.
  • They fuse with the nasal septum growing down.

Congenital anomalies

Source: pages 155 to 157

Clefts of the lip

  • Lateral cleft upper (hare) lip: failure of the maxillary process to fuse with the medial nasal process; unilateral or bilateral.
  • Median (central) cleft upper lip: failure of the medial nasal processes to fuse with each other in the midline above the stomodeum.
  • Median cleft lower lip (very rare): failure of the mandibular processes to fuse with each other in the midline below the stomodeum.

Oblique facial cleft

  • A rare anomaly caused by failure of the maxillary processes to fuse with the lateral nasal process.
  • The nasolacrimal duct is exposed and lies in an opened groove.
  • It is usually associated with a cleft lip on the same side.
Don't confuse

Lateral cleft lip: maxillary process fails to fuse with the medial nasal process. Oblique facial cleft: maxillary process fails to fuse with the lateral nasal process.

Palate and mouth size

  • Cleft palate: failure of the palatine processes of the maxilla to fuse with each other or with the growing-down nasal septum.
  • Microstomia: small mouth.
  • Macrostomia: large mouth.

Check yourself

Source: pages 153 to 157
Which process forms from mesoderm on the under surface of the brain?

The frontonasal process.

In which weeks do the palatine shelves appear and fuse?

They appear in the 6th week and fuse in the midline in the 7th week.

Which fusion fails in an oblique facial cleft?

The maxillary process fails to fuse with the lateral nasal process, exposing the nasolacrimal duct.

Key Points / Quick Review

Facial processes

  • Five masses around the stomodeum at the end of week 4: frontonasal, two maxillary, two mandibular.
  • Nasal placode, then nasal pit, splits the frontonasal process into two lateral nasal and one median nasal process.
  • The globular process is the concave lower lateral edge of the median nasal process.

Fusion

  • Mandibular processes fuse first (lower jaw); maxillary processes follow two weeks later.
  • Globular processes fuse to form the primitive palate.
  • Maxillary processes fuse with each other above the stomodeum to form the definitive upper jaw.
  • Nasolacrimal duct forms from a solid cord in the nasolacrimal groove, then canalizes.

Palate

  • Primitive palate: philtrum, upper incisor-bearing jaw, palate in front of the incisive fossa.
  • Palatine shelves: 6th week; fusion forming the secondary palate: 7th week.

Anomalies

  • Lateral cleft lip: maxillary with medial nasal process.
  • Median cleft upper lip: medial nasal processes; median cleft lower lip: mandibular processes.
  • Oblique facial cleft: maxillary with lateral nasal process.
  • Cleft palate: palatine processes fail to fuse; microstomia small mouth, macrostomia large mouth.
