The bony pelvis: inlet, outlet and divisions

In shortThe pelvic inlet separates the false pelvis above from the true pelvis below, and the outlet closes the pelvis inferiorly.

Two openings define the pelvis: the pelvic inlet (pelvic brim) and the pelvic outlet.

Pelvic inlet

  • In front: upper border of the symphysis pubis and the pubic crest
  • Behind: the sacral promontory
  • Laterally: the pectineal and arcuate lines

Pelvic outlet

  • In front: symphysis pubis and the inferior pubic ligament
  • Behind: the tip of the coccyx
  • Laterally, in front: the ischiopubic ramus
  • Laterally, behind: the ischial tuberosities and the sacrotuberous ligaments

The inlet divides the pelvis into the false (greater) pelvis above and the true (lesser) pelvis below.

False pelvis

  • The expanded part of the pelvic cavity above the plane of the inlet
  • Bounded on either side by the ilium
  • Forms the lower part of the abdominal cavity

True pelvis

  • Lies below and behind the level of the inlet
  • Its bony walls are more complete than those of the false pelvis

Male versus female pelvis

Female pelvis

  • False pelvis: shallow
  • Pelvic inlet: transversely oval
  • Pelvic cavity: wider and shorter
  • Pelvic outlet: larger
  • Ischial tuberosities: everted
  • Sacrum: shorter, flatter and wider
  • Subpubic angle: wider (90° or more)

Male pelvis

  • False pelvis: deep
  • Pelvic inlet: heart shaped
  • Pelvic cavity: narrower
  • Pelvic outlet: smaller
  • Ischial tuberosities: inverted
  • Sacrum: longer, curved and narrower
  • Subpubic angle: narrower (less than 90°)

Position of the pelvis

In the erect position the pelvis lies oblique to the trunk.

  • The plane of the inlet forms an angle of 50° to 60° with the horizontal plane.
  • The plane of the outlet forms an angle of about 15° with the horizontal plane.
  • The anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS) and the upper border of the symphysis pubis lie in the same vertical plane.

Walls of the pelvis

The walls of the pelvis are formed by bones and ligaments, partly closed by muscles covered with fascia and parietal peritoneum. The pelvis has five walls.

Anterior wall
Pelvic surfaces of the bodies of the pubic bones, the pubic rami and the pubic symphysis
Posterior wall
Pelvic surfaces of the sacrum and coccyx, lined by the piriformis muscles with their fascia
Two lateral walls
Bone, ligaments and the obturator internus muscle (next section)
Inferior wall
Pelvic floor (pelvic diaphragm): levator ani and coccygeus muscles

The pelvic diaphragm stretches across the true pelvis and divides it into the main pelvic cavity above and the perineum below.

Muscles of the pelvis

Obturator internus (lateral wall)

Don't confuse

The obturator membrane is attached to the obturator foramen. The obturator fascia is the dense fascia covering the obturator internus muscle.

  • Origin: inner margins of the obturator foramen, the medial surface of the obturator membrane, and the pelvic surface of the ilium between the obturator foramen and the greater sciatic notch.
  • Insertion: medial surface of the greater trochanter, above the trochanteric fossa.
  • Nerve supply: nerve to obturator internus from the sacral plexus (L5, S1, S2).
  • Action: lateral rotator of the femur; helps stabilize the hip joint.
  • The tendinous arch of obturator fascia is a thickening of the fascia running from the back of the body of the pubis to the ischial spine.

Pelvic floor (pelvic diaphragm)

The pelvic floor is formed of two muscles: the levator ani and the coccygeus.

Coccygeus

  • A small muscle lying behind the levator ani.
  • Origin: tip of the ischial spine. Insertion: first piece of the coccyx and last piece of the sacrum.
  • Nerve supply: S4.
  • Action: assists levator ani in supporting the pelvic viscera, and supports the coccyx.

Levator ani

  • Origin: linear origin from the pelvic surface of the body of the pubis, the tendinous arch of obturator fascia and the ischial spine.
  • Its fibers sweep backward, downward and medially and divide into four parts.

Levator prostatae (male) / sphincter vaginae (female)Pass backward and medially across the sides of the prostate or vagina; inserted with their fellows into the perineal body in the median plane

  • Nerve supply: pelvic surface by S4; perineal surface by the perineal branch of the pudendal nerve.
  • Actions: supports the pelvic viscera and guards against prolapse when intra-abdominal pressure rises (cough, labor, constipation); levator prostatae and sphincter vaginae support the prostate and vagina; puborectalis acts as an additional sphincter for the anal canal by keeping the anorectal junction closed except at defecation.

Which two muscles form the pelvic floor?

Key Points / Quick Review

Divisions of the pelvis

  • Inlet: upper symphysis pubis and pubic crest, sacral promontory, pectineal and arcuate lines.
  • Outlet: symphysis pubis and inferior pubic ligament, tip of coccyx, ischiopubic rami, ischial tuberosities, sacrotuberous ligaments.
  • False pelvis above the inlet; true pelvis below and behind it.

Sex differences

  • Female: oval inlet, shallow false pelvis, wider shorter cavity, larger outlet, everted tuberosities, shorter flatter wider sacrum, subpubic angle 90° or more.
  • Male: heart-shaped inlet, deep false pelvis, narrower cavity, smaller outlet, inverted tuberosities, longer curved narrower sacrum, subpubic angle under 90°.

Position and walls

  • Inlet plane 50° to 60°; outlet plane about 15°; ASIS and symphysis pubis share a vertical plane.
  • Five walls: anterior, posterior, two lateral, inferior (pelvic floor).
  • Pelvic diaphragm separates pelvic cavity from perineum.

Muscles

  • Obturator internus: lateral rotator of femur; nerve L5, S1, S2.
  • Coccygeus: ischial spine to coccyx and sacrum; S4; supports viscera and coccyx.
  • Levator ani: four parts; supports viscera and prevents prolapse; puborectalis is an anal sphincter.
