Development of the cord

The cord passes through several stages, starting from the connecting stalk.

  1. Connecting stalk

    The primordium of the cord. It develops at the dorsal pole of the germ disc as a connection between the outer and inner layers of extraembryonic mesoderm, crosses the extraembryonic coelom, and suspends the germ disc, yolk sac and amnion inside the chorionic plate.

  2. Growth of the neural tube

    As the neural tube grows dorsally it pushes the connecting stalk toward the caudal end until it is close to the cloacal membrane.

  3. Folding of the embryo

    The stalk shifts to the ventral surface and lies cranial to the cloacal membrane.

  4. Primitive umbilical cord

    The stalk protrudes through the umbilical ring to form the primitive cord.

  5. Physiological umbilical hernia

    The connecting stalk is surrounded by loops of small intestine (midgut loop), forming the physiological umbilical hernia.

Primitive cord and 10th-week changes

The primitive umbilical cord contains two structures, each surrounded by extraembryonic mesoderm (E.E.M.).

Vitello-intestinal duct
Its E.E.M. gives two vitelline arteries and veins
Allantois (urachus)
Its E.E.M. gives two umbilical arteries and two umbilical veins

Changes at the 10th week

  • The physiological hernia is reduced: the intestinal loops return to the abdominal cavity.
  • The vitello-intestinal duct and the vitelline vessels degenerate.
  • The distal part of the allantois obliterates and forms the median umbilical ligament.
  • The right umbilical vein obliterates.

Definitive umbilical cord

The definitive cord is surrounded by the amnion and contains two umbilical arteries (right and left), one umbilical vein (left), and a jelly-like material called Wharton's jelly that surrounds the vessels.

Characters

  • At birth it is attached between the primitive umbilical ring of the fetus and the center of the placenta.
  • It is 50 to 60 cm long and 2 cm in diameter.
  • It is tortuous and has false nodes, due to unequal growth of the two umbilical arteries.

Functions

Two umbilical arteries

  • Carry venous blood
  • From the fetus to the placenta

Left umbilical vein

  • Carries arterial blood
  • From the placenta to the fetus

The cord also allows the fetus to swim freely in the amniotic cavity.

Changes after birth

  • The left umbilical vein obliterates and forms the ligamentum teres of the liver.
  • The two umbilical arteries obliterate and form the medial umbilical ligaments, which connect the urinary bladder with the umbilicus.

Abnormalities of the umbilical cord

Normally the cord is attached to the center of the fetal surface of the placenta.

AbnormalityDescription / consequence
Long cordMay encircle the neck of the fetus, leading to strangulation
Short cordLeads to premature separation of the placenta
Marginal attachmentCord attached to the margin of the placenta
Velamentous attachmentCord ends before reaching the placenta; the umbilical vessels reach it via the amniotic membrane
False knotResults from kinking of the vessels to accommodate the length of the cord
True knotResults from active fetal movement
OtherDouble umbilical cord, single umbilical artery, true node (in 1% of pregnancies)
Exomphalos (omphalocele)Intestinal loops fail to return to the fetal abdominal cavity and remain herniated in the cord
Congenital umbilical herniaDue to weakness of the abdominal wall
Clinical relevance

In exomphalos the cord should be ligated away from the umbilicus.

Key Points / Quick Review

Development

  • The connecting stalk is the primordium; after folding it lies cranial to the cloacal membrane.
  • The primitive cord contains the vitello-intestinal duct and the allantois (urachus).
  • The physiological hernia is reduced at the 10th week.

Definitive cord

  • Two arteries and one left vein in Wharton's jelly, covered by the amnion.
  • 50 to 60 cm long, 2 cm in diameter.
  • Arteries carry venous blood to the placenta; the vein carries arterial blood to the fetus.

After birth

  • Left umbilical vein becomes the ligamentum teres of the liver.
  • Umbilical arteries become the medial umbilical ligaments.

Abnormalities

  • Long cord: strangulation; short cord: premature placental separation.
  • Marginal and velamentous attachments; false and true knots.
  • Exomphalos: loops fail to return; umbilical hernia: weak abdominal wall.
