Estrogen and progesterone are essential for the physical development of the breasts during pregnancy, but both inhibit the actual secretion of milk.

Prolactin, colostrum and the start of milk secretion

Prolactin concentration rises steadily from the 5th week of pregnancy until birth, reaching 10 to 20 times the non-pregnant level. The placenta also secretes large quantities of human chorionic somatomammotropin, which probably has lactogenic properties and so supports the mother's prolactin.

  1. During pregnancy

    Because of the suppressive effects of estrogen and progesterone, no more than a few milliliters of fluid are secreted each day.

  2. Around parturition

    Colostrum is secreted during the last few days before and the first few days after birth.

  3. Immediately after birth

    There is a sudden loss of both estrogen and progesterone secretion from the placenta.

  4. Over the next 1 to 7 days

    Prolactin from the mother's pituitary takes up its natural milk-promoting role, and the breasts secrete copious milk instead of colostrum.

Definition

Colostrum is the fluid secreted during the last few days before and the first few days after parturition. It has essentially the same concentrations of proteins and lactose as milk, but almost no fat.

Milk ejection (let-down reflex)

Oxytocin causes milk ejection by contraction of the myoepithelial cells that surround the outer walls of the alveoli. Its release is triggered in two ways.

Unconditioned reflex

  • Trigger: suckling
  • Impulses pass via a somatic nerve from the nipples to the spinal cord, then to the hypothalamus
  • The hypothalamus secretes oxytocin

Conditioned reflex

  • Trigger: seeing the baby or thinking of him
  • The cerebral cortex is stimulated
  • It in turn stimulates the hypothalamus to secrete oxytocin

Protection provided by milk

Milk gives the newborn nutrients and also important protection against infection. Several types of white blood cells are secreted in milk, including neutrophils and macrophages, some of which are especially lethal to bacteria that could cause deadly infections in newborns.

Clinical relevance

Antibodies and macrophages in milk destroy Escherichia coli bacteria, which often cause lethal diarrhea in newborns.

Check yourself

Which two factors initiate lactation?

Prolactin and suckling.

Why does copious milk appear only after delivery?

Placental estrogen and progesterone are suddenly lost, so prolactin can promote milk secretion.

How does colostrum differ from milk?

It has the same proteins and lactose concentrations but almost no fat.

Key Points / Quick Review

Milk

  • Initiation: prolactin and suckling
  • 1% protein, 4% fat, 7% lactose; contains type A immunoglobulins

Hormones

  • Estrogens: ducts, stroma, nipples, fat
  • Progesterone: lobules, alveoli, secretory alveolar cells
  • Estrogen and progesterone inhibit actual milk secretion
  • Prolactin forms milk; oxytocin ejects it

After birth

  • Colostrum: almost no fat
  • Loss of placental hormones, then copious milk in 1 to 7 days
  • Let-down: unconditioned (suckling) and conditioned (seeing the baby)
  • Milk protects against infection, including E. coli
