High-yield

The sphenopalatine artery carries the main arterial supply of the nose and is the artery of epistaxis.

Infraorbital artery

It arises from the maxillary artery just before the artery reaches the pterygopalatine fossa, then passes successively through:

Inferior orbital fissure
Infraorbital groove
Infraorbital canal
Infraorbital foramen

It appears on the face through the infraorbital foramen. Its branches are:

  • Branches to the orbital contents.
  • Middle superior alveolar artery: premolar teeth.
  • Anterior superior alveolar artery: descends in the anterior wall of the maxillary sinus and supplies the sinus and the canine and incisor teeth of the upper jaw.
  • On the face: branches to the lacrimal sac, the medial angle of the eye, the side of the nose and the upper lip.

Blood supply of the dura and the middle meningeal artery

The arterial supply of the dura comes from variable sources, mainly the middle and the accessory meningeal arteries. The veins of the dura follow its arteries, and the middle meningeal vein ends in the pterygoid venous plexus.

Course of the middle meningeal artery

  1. Origin

    A branch from the first part of the maxillary artery in the infratemporal fossa.

  2. Entry to the skull

    It ascends and passes through the foramen spinosum, entering the cranial cavity just above the middle of the zygomatic arch.

  3. Intracranial course

    It passes forwards and laterally, grooving the squamous part of the temporal bone.

  4. Division

    About 2 cm above the zygomatic arch it divides into anterior (frontal) and posterior (parietal) branches.

The two terminal branches

Anterior (frontal) branch

  • Ascends on the inner surface of the pterion.
  • Runs parallel to the central sulcus (motor area of the cortex).
  • Surface anatomy: opposite the center of the pterion, 3.5 cm behind the frontozygomatic suture and 4 cm above the zygomatic arch.

Posterior (parietal) branch

  • Passes upwards and backwards towards the lambda, grooving the parietal and temporal bones.
  • Runs nearly over the superior temporal gyrus (auditory area).
  • Surface anatomy: where a horizontal line along the supraorbital margin crosses a vertical line along the posterior border of the mastoid process.
Clinical relevance

Hemorrhage from the anterior branch may compress the motor area. Hemorrhage from the posterior branch may compress the auditory area.

Pterygoid venous plexus

A venous plexus present on the surfaces of the lateral pterygoid muscle or inside the substance of the muscle. Its connections are:

Extracranial

  • With the anterior facial vein, by the deep facial vein.
  • With the supratrochlear and supraorbital veins, by the long ophthalmic veins.

Intracranial

  • With the cavernous sinus, by emissary veins.
Clinical relevance

The plexus may transmit infection from the face to the cavernous sinus.

Key Points / Quick Review

Branches by part

  • First: deep auricular, anterior tympanic, middle meningeal, accessory meningeal, inferior alveolar.
  • Second: muscular branches (pterygoid, masseteric, temporal, buccal).
  • Third: posterior superior alveolar, greater palatine, pterygoid canal, pharyngeal, sphenopalatine, infraorbital.
  • Sphenopalatine is the artery of epistaxis.

Infraorbital artery

  • Inferior orbital fissure, infraorbital groove, infraorbital canal, infraorbital foramen.
  • Anterior superior alveolar: maxillary sinus, canine and incisors. Middle superior alveolar: premolars.

Middle meningeal artery

  • First-part branch; enters through the foramen spinosum; grooves the squamous temporal bone.
  • Divides about 2 cm above the zygomatic arch into anterior and posterior branches.
  • Anterior branch lies near the motor area; posterior branch lies near the auditory area.
  • The middle meningeal vein ends in the pterygoid venous plexus.

Pterygoid venous plexus

  • On or inside the lateral pterygoid muscle.
  • Extracranial: deep facial vein and long ophthalmic veins.
  • Intracranial: emissary veins to the cavernous sinus; may carry facial infection intracranially.
