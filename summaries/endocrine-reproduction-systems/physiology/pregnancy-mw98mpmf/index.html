Fertilization

Source: page 235

Fertilization is the fusion of the sperm with the ovum, which produces the zygote and starts development.

At ovulation the ovum is expelled into the peritoneal cavity, then picked up by the fimbriated end of the uterine tube. Fertilization usually takes place in the ampulla.

  1. Capacitation

    Sperms need 2 to 8 hours in the female genital tract to become more motile and capable of fertilization (sperm capacitation).

  2. Reaching the ovum

    Millions of sperms are deposited in the vagina, but only 50 to 100 reach the ovum. About 10 of these adhere to the zona pellucida at specific sperm receptors.

  3. Acrosomal reaction

    The acrosomal reaction is the breakdown of the acrosome at the sperm head. It releases enzymes, including acrosin (a trypsin-like protease) and hyaluronidase, which help the sperm penetrate the zona pellucida.

  4. Fusion with the ovum

    After penetrating the zona pellucida, the sperm head adheres to the ovum cell membrane. The area of fusion breaks down and the sperm nucleus is released into the ovum cytoplasm.

  5. Zygote and morula

    This fusion signals the start of development and gives the zygote. After about a day it divides mitotically to form a morula, which moves down the uterine tube and reaches the uterus within 3 to 4 days of fertilization.

Functions of the placenta

Source: pages 236 to 237

1. Selective exchange

  • Transport of nutrients to the fetus.
  • Exchange of respiratory gases.
  • Acts as a kidney in the excretion of waste products.

2. Protection

The placenta is an efficient barrier against the passage of most bacteria and toxins from the maternal blood to the fetus. It is permeable to most antitoxins and antibodies, particularly immunoglobulin G (IgG).

3. Endocrine organ

The placenta is an endocrine organ for maintenance of pregnancy and preparation for labor and lactation.

Physiologic changes during pregnancy

Amenorrhea (first sign)
Feedback inhibition of pituitary GTHs by high blood levels of estrogen and progesterone
Morning sickness (first trimester)
Probably over secretion of thyroxine by hCG and hC thyrotropin
Acromegalic face
Probably the effect of hCS
Increased BMR
Excessive thyroxine and glucocorticoids, and high sex hormone levels
Ankle edema (late)
Enlarged uterus obstructs blood flow from the lower limbs
Weight gain
Fetus, membranes, amniotic fluid, placenta, enlarged uterus and breasts, and increased blood volume
Increased appetite and food intake
Supplies the increased nutritional demand of the growing fetus
Increased pulmonary ventilation
Supplies extra O₂ needed by the fetus and mother because of the increased metabolic rate

Other changes listed:

  • Enlargement of the internal and external sex organs and the breasts.
  • Increase in blood volume, venous return and cardiac output.
  • Mild increase in urine volume and frequency of micturition.

Check yourself

How long do sperms need in the female genital tract before they can fertilize the ovum?

About 2 to 8 hours (capacitation).

Which placental hormone has luteinizing and luteotropic actions like pituitary LH?

hCG, secreted by the syncytiotrophoblast and then the chorionic villi.

What is the first sign of pregnancy, and what causes it?

Amenorrhea, caused by feedback inhibition of pituitary GTHs by high estrogen and progesterone.

Key Points / Quick Review

Fertilization

  • Usual site: ampulla of the uterine tube.
  • Capacitation takes 2 to 8 hours; 50 to 100 sperms reach the ovum.
  • Acrosomal reaction releases acrosin and hyaluronidase.
  • The morula reaches the uterus within 3 to 4 days.

Placental functions

  • Selective exchange (nutrients, gases, excretion), protection (IgG crosses) and endocrine function.

Placental hormones

  • Estrogens sensitize the myometrium to oxytocin; progesterone decreases that sensitivity.
  • hCG maintains the corpus luteum for the first 6 to 8 weeks.
  • HCS is the growth hormone of pregnancy; relaxin softens pelvic ligaments.

Maternal changes

  • Amenorrhea is the first sign; BMR, blood volume, venous return, cardiac output and ventilation increase.
  • Late ankle edema is due to obstruction of venous flow by the enlarged uterus.

Endocrine changes

  • Pituitary enlarges (more ACTH and prolactin) while GTHs and TSH are inhibited.
  • More glucocorticoids, aldosterone, parathormone and calcitonin.
